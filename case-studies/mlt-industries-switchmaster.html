Remote concept-to-manufacture case study

MLT Industries: from an early product concept to manufacture and a growing product family.

This project is a useful example of what end-to-end product design can look like when the client, designer, prototype suppliers, and manufacturers are not in the same place. Orion worked from Belfast with MLT Industries in Australia, connecting the physical product journey from concept through prototypes, supplier sourcing, manufacture, and later product-family development.

Client
MLT Industries, Australia
Orion base
Belfast, Northern Ireland
Delivery model
Remote collaboration + international suppliers
Scope
Concept → prototype → manufacture → family

The project journey

One relationship across the stages where product decisions become expensive.

Many product-development engagements cover one slice of the process. The value of the MLT relationship is continuity: early design intent could be carried forward into prototype learning, manufacturing choices, and later extensions rather than being repeatedly reinterpreted at each hand-off.

01

Concept definition

The work began by turning an early product idea into a physical architecture that could be discussed, challenged, and developed rather than remaining an abstract intention.

02

Prototype and learn

Prototype iterations created physical evidence: fit, access, assembly, usability, packaging of the electronics, and the practical questions that are difficult to resolve convincingly in CAD alone.

03

Design for manufacture and supplier sourcing

As the product matured, the design conversation moved toward manufacturing processes, production detail, and finding suitable prototype and manufacturing suppliers internationally. The objective was not simply to find a supplier, but to make sure the product and process assumptions made sense together.

04

Manufacture

The project progressed beyond presentation models into a manufactured product. That transition is where design details such as fastening, access, cable routing, enclosure construction, repeatability, and assembly burden stop being theoretical.

05

Extend the platform

The relationship continued as the original product developed into a broader family. Reusing an established product language and manufacturing logic can reduce the amount that has to be rediscovered when a successful product grows into variants and accessories.

Why this case matters

Remote does not have to mean fragmented.

Good remote product development depends on clear models, drawings, decisions, photographs, prototype feedback, and supplier communication. Geography changes the communication system; it does not change the engineering fundamentals.

Public outcome

A distributed development model

Client, designer, prototype suppliers, and manufacturers can operate as one product-development system.

AustraliaClient, product need, market context, field feedback
↔
BelfastProduct design, mechanical development, coordination
↔
International suppliersPrototype manufacture, process feedback, production

The important interface is not distance. It is the quality of the information crossing each boundary: what changed, why it changed, which assumptions remain open, and what evidence the next prototype or supplier interaction needs to produce.
