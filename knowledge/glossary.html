By Andrew Hunter, Product Design Engineer · Last reviewed 27 November 2026 · 350 terms

Product design and manufacturing glossary.

Plain-English definitions of the terms you will meet when developing, making and selling a physical product: from prototypes and tooling to landed cost, funding and certification. Where a term is explained in more depth on Orion Design, follow the link.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWY0–9

A

ABS
Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene: a common, low-cost rigid plastic with a good surface finish, widely used for enclosures and consumer products. More
Acceptable quality limit (AQL)
The defect level used with a sampling standard such as ISO 2859-1 to decide whether a production batch passes inspection. More
Acetal (POM)
Polyoxymethylene: a stiff, low-friction engineering plastic used for gears, clips and mechanisms. More
Aluminium tool
An injection-mould tool machined from aluminium: quicker and cheaper to make than steel, but it wears sooner, so it suits prototypes and lower volumes. More
Amortisation
Spreading a one-off cost, such as tooling, across the units it helps produce, so it can be included in the unit cost. More
Angel investor
An individual who invests their own money in an early-stage company, usually in exchange for shares. More
Anisotropy
Having different properties in different directions. A 3D-printed part, especially one printed by FDM, is often weaker between its layers than along them. More
Anodising
A process that grows a hard oxide layer out of the surface of a metal, usually aluminium. It can be clear or dyed, resists wear and corrosion, does not chip like paint and is electrically insulating. More
Assembly time
The labour time needed to put a product together, which drives assembly cost on every unit. More

B

Batch production
Making a product in discrete quantities rather than continuously, typical for start-ups and lower-volume products.
Bead blasting
Firing fine glass beads or similar media at a part to give an even matt texture that hides machining marks, often before anodising. More
Bending stress
The stress in a part as it bends, highest at the surface furthest from the middle of the section and where the bending moment is largest, such as the root of a cantilever bracket. Compared with the material's yield or allowable stress, it shows whether the part will spring back or take a permanent bend. More
Bill of materials (BOM)
The structured list of every part, material and quantity needed to make a product, used for costing, purchasing and assembly. More
Billet
A cylinder of aluminium, heated until it is soft but still solid, that a press pushes through the die to make an extrusion. More
Board-to-board connector
A connector that joins two circuit boards directly, stacked or at right angles, so they need no cable and locate each other. The housing must still hold both boards so the connector does not carry the load. More
Boss
A raised cylindrical feature on a moulded part that receives a screw, pin or insert. More
Brand registry
A programme run by some large online marketplaces that helps brand owners manage their listings and report copies. Such programmes generally require a trade mark, registered or applied for, in the relevant country. More
Brazing
Joining metal parts with a filler metal that melts at a lower temperature than the parts themselves. It distorts parts less than welding and can join different metals, but is permanent. More
Break-even point
The sales volume at which total contribution covers fixed costs, so the product neither makes nor loses money. More
Bridge tooling
Lower-cost, faster tooling used to make early production parts while demand and design settle, before committing to full production tooling. More
Buffer stock
Extra stock of a part or product held in reserve, so a late delivery or a sudden rise in demand does not stop production or sales. More
Burn rate
How quickly a business spends its cash each month before it is profitable. More
Business case
A structured argument that a product is worth developing, bringing together market, price, cost, investment and risk. More

C

Cable gland
A fitting that clamps a seal around a cable where it passes through the wall of a housing, so water and dust cannot follow the cable in. It suits round cables of a known diameter. More
CAD
Computer-aided design: software used to create the 3D models and drawings that define a product.
Cantilever snap fit
The most common type of snap fit: a hook on the end of a short flexible beam that bends as the parts are pushed together and springs back behind a catch. The strain at the root of the beam must stay below the material's permissible strain. More
Capacitive touch
A touch control with no moving parts that works through a solid wall, so it seals well and has nothing to wear. It needs light, sound or vibration as feedback, and gloves or water can stop it working reliably. More
Carbon footprint
The greenhouse gas emissions linked to a product across the life cycle stages counted, usually expressed as carbon dioxide equivalent. It measures one impact, where a life cycle assessment looks at many. More
Case quantity
The number of units packed in each outer case, often chosen to suit how a retailer orders. Changing it changes the case size and how cases fit on a pallet. More
Cash flow
The movement of money into and out of a business over time. A profitable business can still run short of cash if money goes out before it comes in. More
Cash low point
The lowest point of a business's cumulative cash forecast, which sets the minimum funding it needs. More
Cavity
The hollow in an injection-mould tool that forms the part. A multi-cavity tool makes several parts per cycle. More
CE marking
The conformity marking used for many products sold in the EU and in Northern Ireland, and recognised in Great Britain for many product types. More
Change control
A simple, recorded process for approving design changes once a design is frozen, because changes then affect tools, suppliers and test reports. More
Channel margin
The share of the retail price kept by a retailer, distributor or marketplace. More
Classes of goods and services
The groups into which goods and services are divided when a trade mark is registered. A registration covers particular goods and services, so the same word can be registered by different owners for unrelated goods. More
Clearance fit
A fit in which the shaft is always smaller than the hole, so there is always a gap. Used for parts that must slide, turn or assemble by hand. More
Clinching
Joining overlapping metal sheets by pressing them into an interlocking button, with no heat, fasteners or consumables. The joint is permanent. More
CNC machining
Computer-controlled cutting of parts from solid material, used for prototypes, precise parts and low volumes. More
COGS
Cost of goods sold: the direct cost of making and delivering each unit sold. More
Cold-chamber machine
A die-casting machine in which metal is ladled from a separate furnace into a shot sleeve, then a plunger drives it into the die. It is used for aluminium, which would attack parts left sitting in it, and for much magnesium. More
Colour, material and finish (CMF)
The visible colours, materials, textures and surface finishes chosen for a product, and how they look, feel and age. CMF is usually led by industrial design. More
Commodity code
The classification number that sets the duty rate and import rules for a product. More
Concept selection
Choosing between design concepts using structured criteria rather than preference. More
Conformal coating
A thin protective film applied over an assembled circuit board to help it resist moisture and condensation. It is lighter and less permanent than potting. More
Conformity assessment
The process of showing a product meets the regulations that apply to it, by self-assessment or through an independent body. More
Contingency
Money set aside in a budget for problems that will happen but cannot yet be named; commonly 15 to 25 per cent in hardware development. More
Contract manufacturer
A company that sources parts, assembles, tests and packs a product to the customer's design. More
Contribution
Selling price received minus the variable costs of each sale: the amount each unit contributes to fixed costs and profit. More
Copyright
Automatic protection for original works such as drawings, manuals, software and photographs. More
Core
The part of an injection-mould tool that forms the inside surfaces of a moulded part. More
Coring out
Hollowing a thick feature of a moulded part so it is made of walls close to the nominal wall thickness. It avoids sink marks and voids, and shortens cooling time. More
Cosmetic face
A face of a part that people will see or touch, marked on the drawing so that suppliers keep marks, defects and support or contact points off it and finish it to the agreed standard. More
Cost-down
Lowering the cost of making a product that is already in production, for example by removing parts, operations or unneeded precision. Each change needs validating before it reaches production. More
Cradle to gate
A footprint boundary covering raw materials and manufacturing, up to the product leaving the factory. More
Cradle to grave
A footprint boundary covering a product's whole life: materials, manufacturing, distribution, use and end of life. More
Creep
The slow, continuing deformation of a part under a load that stays on, such as a screw boss or a snap fit left bent. It matters most for plastics and is faster when they are warm, so use long-term (creep) modulus data for a sustained load. More
Crimped terminal
A terminal fixed to the end of a wire by squeezing it on with dedicated crimp tooling. Harness suppliers have the tooling and test equipment to do this consistently, which is one reason to buy made-up harnesses. More
Critical to quality (CTQ)
A dimension, function or property that decides whether a product works, is safe or is acceptable to the customer. CTQ features are marked on drawings and checked more closely than the rest. More
Crowdfunding
Raising money from many people, often as pre-orders or rewards, before a product is made. More
Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
The average sales and marketing spend needed to win one new customer. More
Customer discovery
Talking to people who have a problem, about how they deal with it today, before designing a product to solve it. More
Customs value
The value on which import duty is calculated, usually the price paid plus transport and insurance to the UK border. More
Cutting die
A one-off tool, often called a cutter, that cuts and creases sheets of card into the shape of a particular box. Changing the box shape usually means a new cutter. More
Cycle time
The time taken to make one shot or one part on a machine, a key driver of part cost. More

D

Datum
A feature, such as a face, edge, hole or axis, that a drawing declares as the reference from which measurements start. Datums are usually the features that locate the part in an assembly. More
DDP
Delivered duty paid: an Incoterms rule under which the seller delivers to the buyer's premises with duty paid. More
Declaration of conformity
A signed statement by the manufacturer that a product meets the regulations that apply to it. More
Deflection
How far a part moves when it bends under load, such as the drop at the tip of a bracket. For a simple beam it grows with the load and the cube of the length, and falls as the elastic modulus and second moment of area rise. More
Descriptive mark
A name that simply describes a product or its qualities. Such names are generally hard to protect as trade marks, because other traders need to be free to use ordinary descriptive words; distinctive or invented names tend to be stronger. More
Design for assembly (DFA)
Designing a product so it is quick and reliable to put together, for example with fewer parts and fasteners. More
Design for manufacture (DFM)
Designing parts so they can be made reliably and economically by the chosen process. More
Design freeze
The point at which a design is fixed and further changes go through change control. More
Design review
A short, structured meeting where the right people check a design against its requirements and risks, then decide whether it is ready to move to the next stage: proceed, proceed once named actions are closed, or rework. More
Design right
In the UK, automatic protection against copying of the shape and configuration of a product. More
Design verification
Testing production-intent parts against the requirements before committing to production. More
Designated standard
In Great Britain, a standard that gives a presumption of conformity with the relevant product regulations when it is met. More
Development board
An off-the-shelf circuit board carrying a processor, radio or sensors with easy connections, used to prove electronics and firmware before a custom board is designed. More
Die casting
Forcing molten metal, often aluminium or zinc, into a steel die to make metal parts at volume. More
Digital product passport
Product information on materials, repair and recycling that the EU plans to require for some product groups under its ecodesign framework. More
Direct to consumer (DTC)
Selling directly to end customers, usually through your own website, without a retailer. More
Distributor
A business that buys products in bulk and sells them on to retailers or businesses, taking its own margin. More
Draft angle
The slight taper on faces parallel to the tool opening that lets a moulded part release cleanly. More
Duty
A tax charged on imported goods, at a rate set by the product's commodity code and origin. More

E

Ecodesign
Requirements for the environmental performance of products, such as energy-related products, over their life. More
Effectivity
The point from which a change applies, given as a date, a production batch or a serial number. Recording it means you can always tell which units contain the old part and which contain the new one. More
Ejector pin
A pin in an injection-mould tool that pushes the finished part out; it leaves a small mark. More
Elastic modulus
A material's stiffness, written E and given in MPa on its datasheet; also called Young's modulus. With the second moment of area it sets how far a part bends, and for plastics under a load that stays on, the long-term (creep) modulus applies. More
Electromagnetic compatibility (EMC)
The ability of electrical equipment to work without causing or suffering unacceptable electromagnetic interference. More
Embodied carbon
The greenhouse gas emissions from making a product's materials and parts, before it is used. More
End cap
A plate or cap that closes an open end of an extruded enclosure and carries its connectors, lights and seal, usually screwed into the screw ports with a gasket against the cut end. Machined aluminium end plates suit low volumes; moulded plastic caps need a mould, so they usually come later. More
Engineering BOM (EBOM)
The bill of materials for the product as designed. It usually mirrors the assembly structure in the CAD model and is owned by the design team. More
Engineering change request (ECR)
A short, numbered record of a proposed change to a released part: what should change, why, and which part numbers are affected. It then carries the impact check, the approval decision and the effectivity, so the whole history of the change is in one place. More
Engineering prototype
A prototype that integrates form and function to check the design against its specification. More
Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS)
A UK scheme offering tax reliefs to investors in qualifying smaller companies. More
Equity
Ownership in a company; equity funding means selling shares in exchange for investment. More
Ergonomics
Designing a product to fit the people who use it: grip, reach, weight, balance, forces and comfort across the range of intended users. More
Evidence ladder
A way of ranking market evidence from weak (opinions) to strong (payments and commitments). More
EVT, DVT and PVT
Engineering, design and production validation testing: the last three development stages, as named in many electronics teams. More
Extended producer responsibility (EPR)
Rules that make producers responsible for the cost of managing their products or packaging at end of life. More
Extrusion
A process that pushes material through a shaped die to make long parts with a constant cross-section, cut to length. More
Extrusion die
The steel disc, with the profile's shape cut through it, that heated aluminium is pushed through to make an extrusion. It is relatively simple, so it usually costs much less than a die-casting die or injection mould for a similar part, though cost rises with profile size, complexity and the number of hollows. More
EXW
Ex works: an Incoterms rule under which the buyer takes responsibility for the goods from the seller's premises. More

F

Face seal
An O-ring seal in which the ring sits in a groove in a flat face and is squeezed as the two faces are pulled together, usually by screws. It depends on the faces closing fully and evenly. More
Failure mode
The way in which a product or part fails, such as cracking, wearing, leaking or not switching on. Recording failures by mode makes patterns and their causes easier to spot. More
Family tool
An injection-mould tool that makes several different parts in one cycle. More
FDM
Fused deposition modelling: 3D printing by extruding melted plastic filament layer by layer. More
Field trial
Putting a small batch of products into real use to learn how they perform over time. More
Financial model
A spreadsheet that links unit cost, price, volume, fixed costs and payment timing to show profit, break-even and how much funding a business needs. More
Finite element analysis (FEA)
Computer simulation of stresses, deflections or heat in a design, used to check it before building.
Firm order
An order that commits the buyer to pay, as distinct from a forecast, which only helps the supplier plan. A supply agreement can say when orders become firm and whether the buyer pays for materials bought against a forecast. More
Firmware
The software built into a product's electronics that controls how the hardware behaves. More
First article inspection
A detailed check of the first production parts against the drawings to confirm the process is right. More
Fixed costs
Costs that do not change with the number of units sold, such as tooling, design and overheads. More
Fixture (jig)
A holder that locates parts in the right position while they are assembled, glued, pressed or tested, so the operator no longer judges alignment by eye. A part that will not sit in its fixture is probably wrong or the wrong way round. More
Flash
Thin fins of plastic along the parting line or around inserts and ejectors of a moulded part, usually caused by too much pressure or tool steel that does not seal. More
Flexible printed circuit (FPC)
A thin, flat, flexible circuit that links boards at different angles or on moving parts, and is the same in every unit. It needs a defined bend shape with no sharp creases, and care at assembly because its connectors are small and easy to fit at an angle. More
FOB
Free on board: an Incoterms rule under which the seller pays to load the goods onto the ship at the origin port. More
Food contact materials
Materials and parts intended to touch food or drink, such as containers, utensils and appliance parts. They need grades and evidence suited to that use, so check the rules early. More
Form, fit and function
The usual test of whether a changed part is interchangeable with the old one. If it can still replace the old part everywhere it is used without any other change, it gets a new revision; if not, it gets a new part number. More
Form-in-place gasket
A gasket made by dispensing a bead of liquid onto a flange by machine, which cures into a soft seal on the part. It follows complex shapes, but needs dispensing equipment and a groove or stop to set the compression. More
Freedom to operate
Whether a product can be made and sold without infringing other people's patents or rights. More
Fulfilment
Storing, picking, packing and delivering orders to customers, done yourself or by a third party such as a marketplace. More
Functional unit
The basis a footprint or LCA compares on, such as one year of use, so products with different lifetimes compare fairly. More

G

Galvanic corrosion
Corrosion that happens when two different metals touch and are bridged by water, particularly salt water: the less noble metal, such as aluminium or zinc, corrodes faster. Using one metal throughout, or insulating the joint and keeping water out, prevents it. More
Gasket
A soft rubber or foam seal squeezed between two parts so it fills the small gaps between them. It needs a stiff flange and even, controlled compression to seal reliably. More
Gate
The point where plastic enters the cavity of an injection-mould tool; it leaves a small mark. More
General tolerance
The tolerance that applies to every dimension on a drawing that has none of its own, usually stated in the title block or by reference to a standard such as ISO 2768. More
Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T)
A system of drawing symbols that controls the shape, orientation and location of features, such as the flatness of a face or the position of a hole, measured from datums. It complements plus-or-minus size tolerances. More
Golden sample
An approved reference unit that production parts are compared against. More
Grommet
A fitting in a hole through a wall that protects a cable from the edge of the hole. Where the product must be sealed, a cable gland or a sealed connector keeps water out. More
Gross margin
Revenue minus the cost of goods sold, often expressed as a percentage of revenue. More
GS1
The organisation that runs the barcode numbering system retailers commonly use. A business obtains its GTINs through membership of GS1 in its own country; in the UK that is GS1 UK. More
GTIN
Global Trade Item Number: the number behind a retail barcode, identifying one specific product to tills and stock systems. Each variant a shopper could buy needs its own. More
Gusset
A triangular rib that supports a boss or wall in a moulded part. More

H

Hang tab
A tab with a hole or slot at the top of a retail pack so it can hang from a peg or hook in a shop. It must be strong enough for handling and let the pack hang straight. More
Hardened steel tool
An injection-mould tool made from hardened steel for long life and high volumes. More
Heat checking
Fine surface cracks that form on a die-casting die as every shot heats it sharply and it cools again. They show as raised lines on castings until the die is repaired. More
Heat sink
Finned metal, usually aluminium, fixed to a hot component to give it more surface area to lose heat. It needs moving air around it, so it suits vented products better than small sealed ones. More
Heat-set insert
A threaded metal insert, usually brass, pressed into a hole in a plastic part with a heated tip, so the melted plastic flows into its knurls and holds it. It gives a thread that survives repeated assembly. More
Hollow and semi-hollow profiles
A hollow profile fully encloses at least one space, like a tube, so its die needs a mandrel supported on bridges; a solid profile, like a flat bar, encloses none. A semi-hollow profile nearly encloses a space through a narrow gap, like a C-shaped screw port, and can be harder to make than it looks because the thin tongue of die steel forming the gap is weak. More
Hot runner
A heated channel system in a mould that keeps plastic molten up to the cavity, reducing waste and sometimes cycle time. More
Hot-chamber machine
A die-casting machine whose injection mechanism sits in the molten metal, so a plunger pushes each shot straight into the die and cycles are short. It is usually used for zinc and other low-melting alloys, and some magnesium. More

I

Import VAT
VAT charged on imported goods, normally reclaimable by VAT-registered businesses. More
Impregnation
A treatment that seals the porosity in a casting so it cannot leak. For die-cast parts that must seal, ask the die caster about it alongside leak testing. More
In-mould decoration
Placing a printed film in an injection-mould tool so it is moulded into the part, giving graphics that do not wear off. More
Inclusive design
Designing a product so it can be used by people with a wide range of abilities, ages and circumstances. Designing for the edges of the user group often makes a product easier for everyone. More
Incoterms
International rules, published by the International Chamber of Commerce, that define who pays for transport, insurance and duty in a sale. More
Indented BOM
A bill of materials laid out in levels, with parts grouped under their sub-assemblies and quantities given per parent. It shows what a change touches, and a flat list of total quantities can be produced from it. More
Industrial design
Design focused on a product's form, use and appearance. More
Injection moulding
Making plastic parts by injecting molten plastic into a machined metal tool, cooling it and ejecting the part. More
Inner pack
An optional level of packaging that groups a few unit packs together inside an outer case, to make handling and counting easier. More
Innovate UK
The UK's innovation agency, which runs competitions for innovation funding. More
Insert
A part, often a threaded metal bush, placed into a plastic part by heat, ultrasound or moulding-in.
Intellectual property (IP)
Creations such as inventions, designs, brands and original written or drawn work that the law can protect, through patents, design rights, trade marks and copyright. More
Interference fit
A fit in which the shaft is always larger than the hole, so the parts must be pressed or shrunk together. Used where parts must stay put without fasteners, such as bearings and dowels. More
Invest NI
Northern Ireland's economic development agency, which supports innovation and business growth. More
IP rating
Ingress protection rating: a code, such as IP54 or IP67, summarising how well an enclosure keeps out solids and water in standard tests. The first digit covers solid objects and dust, the second water; an X means that aspect was not tested. More
ISO 2768
An international standard for general tolerances, often quoted on drawings for dimensions that have no individual tolerance. More

K

Keep-out zone
An area of a circuit board or enclosure where components, tracks or metal must not go, for example under a mounting boss or around an antenna. More
Key performance indicator (KPI)
A measure chosen to track progress towards a business goal, such as gross margin, sales growth or runway. More

L

Labyrinth
Overlapping walls that force water to take a winding path into a housing, keeping out splashes and rain without a seal. It does not wear out, but will not stop water under pressure or immersion. More
Landed cost
The total cost of getting a product from the supplier to your warehouse ready to sell. More
Layer lines
The visible ridges left on a 3D-printed part where each layer was built on the one below. They affect the look and feel of the part and, in FDM, where it is weakest. More
Lead time
The time between placing an order and receiving the goods. More
Lead-in angle
The sloping face of a snap-fit hook that rides over the catch as the parts are pushed together, also called the assembly angle. A shallower slope makes assembly easier but needs a longer hook. More
Life cycle assessment (LCA)
A standard method for assessing a product's environmental impacts across its whole life. More
Lifetime value (LTV)
The gross profit a business expects from a customer over the whole relationship, including repeat purchases, consumables and accessories. More
Lifter
A moving part in an injection-mould tool that releases an internal undercut as the part is ejected. More
Light pipe
A clear plastic part that carries light from an LED on a circuit board to the outside of an enclosure. More
Limit sample
A part kept to show the worst acceptable version of a cosmetic feature, such as the largest acceptable gate mark. Production parts are compared with it, so cosmetic decisions do not depend on memory or opinion. More
Living hinge
A thin, flexible section of plastic that acts as a hinge, typically moulded in polypropylene. More
Looks-like prototype
A model that shows a product's size, shape and appearance, but may not work. More

M

Manufacturing BOM (MBOM)
The bill of materials for the product as built, grouped by how the factory assembles it. It adds things CAD does not show, such as glue, grease, protective film and packing materials. More
Marketplace
An online platform where third parties sell to consumers in exchange for commission and fees. More
Markup
Profit as a share of cost: (price minus cost) divided by cost. More
Masking
Covering areas of a part, such as threads, fits and earthing points, so a coating, plating or anodising does not reach them. Masked areas should be few, simple and shown on the drawing. More
Masterbatch
A concentrated mix of pigment or additive in a carrier plastic, blended with natural plastic at the moulding machine to colour or modify the part. More
Match funding
A requirement that a grant recipient contributes part of the project cost from its own funds. More
Material certificate
A document from a material supplier confirming the grade, batch and key properties of the material supplied. It helps trace and check what went into a part. More
Membrane switch
A flat switch panel bonded to the face of a product, usually under a printed overlay that carries the graphics on its back face. It seals the face in one piece and needs little tooling; metal domes can add a click. More
Membrane vent
A small patch of microporous membrane on a sealed housing that lets air through but blocks liquid water, so the pressure changes as the housing heats and cools do not strain the seal. More
Minimum order quantity (MOQ)
The smallest order a supplier will accept. More
MJF
Multi Jet Fusion: 3D printing that fuses fine plastic powder, usually nylon, layer by layer, giving tough functional parts without support structures. More
Mould tool
The machined metal tool that shapes parts in injection moulding or casting. More
Mould-flow analysis
Simulation of how plastic fills a mould, used to predict problems such as short shots, weld lines and warping. More
Moulded texture
A matt or patterned surface made by texturing the surface of the mould tool, so every part comes out with it. Textured faces usually need more draft than plain ones. More
Moulder
A company that runs injection-moulding machines to make parts from a mould tool. It may also make the tool, or work with a separate toolmaker. More
Multi-cavity tool
A mould tool that makes several identical parts per cycle, lowering cost per part at higher tool cost. More

N

NDA
Non-disclosure agreement: a contract that keeps shared information confidential. More
Net price
The price after VAT, discounts and channel deductions: what the business actually receives. More
Nominal wall
The standard wall thickness a moulded part is designed around. More
Nylon (PA)
Polyamide: a strong, tough engineering plastic that absorbs moisture, often glass-filled for stiffness. More

O

O-ring
A moulded rubber ring that sits in a groove and seals when squeezed between two parts. Because the groove controls the squeeze, it is one of the most dependable seals, and standard sizes are widely available. More
ODM
Original design manufacturer: a company that designs and makes products, often adapting an existing design for a client. More
OEM
Original equipment manufacturer: a company whose products or parts are sold under another brand or built into another product.
Outer case
The shipping carton that holds a number of retail packs, usually with its own label and sometimes its own barcode. Retail stock usually moves in outer cases on pallets. More
Overflow
A small pocket beside the cavity of a die-casting die that collects the first, coldest metal and some trapped air. It is trimmed off the casting with the runner and flash. More
Overheads
Running costs that are not tied to individual units, such as salaries, rent and software. More
Overmoulding
Moulding one material, often a soft TPE, over another part to add grip, seals or colour. More

P

Pad printing
Printing that transfers ink from an etched plate to a part with a soft silicone pad, so logos and markings can be applied to curved or recessed surfaces. More
Part consolidation
Combining two or more parts that never move relative to each other into one, removing a part, a joint and an assembly step. The new part may need a new or modified tool. More
Part count
The number of separate parts in a product. Each one adds tooling or set-up, stock, handling and assembly time, so reducing it is a core aim of design for assembly. More
Parting line
The line where the two halves of a mould meet; it can leave a visible mark on the part. More
Passivation
A chemical treatment for stainless steel that removes surface contamination, such as iron from tools, so the steel's own corrosion resistance works as intended. It barely changes the look of the part. More
Patent
A registered right that protects how a new invention works, for a limited time, in return for public disclosure. More
Payback period
The time taken for cumulative returns to repay an investment. More
Payment terms
The agreed timing and conditions of payment between buyer and seller, such as a deposit with the order and the balance before shipment, or payment a set number of days after invoice. More
PC/ABS
A blend of polycarbonate and ABS that balances toughness, heat resistance and surface finish. More
Permissible strain
The most strain a plastic can take in a flexing feature, such as a snap fit, without cracking or staying bent, taken from the material supplier's design data for the exact grade. Suppliers often give a lower figure for a joint opened many times than for one assembled once. More
Pilot production
A small production run on the real process to prove it can make the product repeatably. More
Pin-out
The record of which wire runs from which pin to which at each connector, usually given as a table on the harness drawing. Every harness should be checked for continuity and correct pin-out in production. More
Plating
Depositing a thin layer of metal, such as zinc, nickel or chrome, onto a part for corrosion protection, wear resistance or a decorative metal look. More
Poka-yoke (error-proofing)
A Japanese term for error-proofing: arranging the design, fixture or process so a mistake cannot be made, or is caught straight away. Asymmetric parts that only fit one way and keyed connectors are common examples. More
Polycarbonate (PC)
A very tough, heat-resistant plastic available in transparent grades. More
Polypropylene (PP)
A light, tough, chemical-resistant plastic often used for containers and living hinges. More
Porosity
Small pores inside a casting, from trapped gas and from shrinkage where thick sections freeze last; every die casting has some. Machining can open pores into a leak path on a sealing face or weak engagement in a thread, so machined features are kept in even sections. More
Potting
Filling the space around electronics with resin that sets solid. It protects well against water and vibration, but adds weight and makes the board unrepairable. More
Powder coating
A finish in which a dry powder is sprayed onto a metal part and baked into a tough, even coat. It comes in many colours and textures, builds up thicker than paint and is electrically insulating. More
Pre-certified radio module
A ready-made radio module that has already been tested as a module. It reduces the radio testing a product needs, but it does not certify the whole product and its antenna guidance must still be followed. More
Pre-compliance testing
Informal testing during development to find compliance problems before formal certification tests. More
Pre-order
An order and payment taken before a product is available, used to fund and test demand. More
Pre-shipment inspection
A check of a sample from a finished, packed batch before it leaves the factory, against an agreed standard and sampling plan. More
Price break
A quantity at which a supplier's unit price steps down. Asking every supplier for the same price breaks makes their quotes comparable. More
Printed circuit board (PCB)
The board that holds and connects a product's electronic components through copper tracks. Once the components are fitted it is often called a PCB assembly (PCBA). More
Product liability
The legal responsibility of producers for harm caused by defective products. More
Product requirements document (PRD)
A written list of what a product must do, for whom, with measurable targets and constraints. More
Profile (extrusion)
The cross-section of an extrusion, and the lengths of aluminium made with it. An off-the-shelf enclosure profile needs no tooling; a custom profile needs its own die. More
Proof of principle
An early build that tests whether a product's core idea works. More
Prototype
An early version of a product built to answer a specific question about it. More
Pugh matrix
A concept-selection method that scores options against a reference design, criterion by criterion. More
Purchase order
A formal order from a buyer to a supplier stating items, quantities, prices and terms. More

Q

Quality plan
An agreed description of inspections, acceptance criteria and responsibilities for making a product. More
Quote validity
How long a supplier's quoted price holds, and the conditions, such as changes in material prices, under which it may be revised. More

R

R&D tax relief
UK Corporation Tax relief on qualifying research and development spending. More
Radial seal
An O-ring seal in which the ring sits around a plug or spigot and is squeezed against the wall of a bore as one part slides into the other. The fit sets the squeeze, which suits round lids and end caps. More
Ramp-up
The period after a successful pilot run when production builds up from first batches to full volume, while any remaining actions are closed. More
Recycled content
The share of a product or material made from recycled sources. More
Registered design
A registered right protecting the appearance of a product. More
Registered trade mark (®)
A trade mark registered for particular goods and services in a particular country or region. The ® symbol indicates a registered mark and should only be used once it is registered, and only where it is registered; ™ can be used with a name you treat as a trade mark whether or not it is registered. More
Request for quotation (RFQ)
A request to suppliers for a price, sent with drawings, quantities and requirements. More
Retail price (RRP)
Recommended retail price: the price the end customer is expected to pay, usually including VAT in the UK. More
Retention angle
The face of a snap-fit hook that holds the parts together once assembled. At or near 90° to the pull the joint cannot be pulled apart; a shallower angle lets the hook slide out under a firm pull. More
Revision
A recorded version of a part, drawing or bill of materials, usually marked with a letter such as Rev B. Each change raises the revision, so everyone can tell exactly which version they are working from. More
Rib
A thin wall added to a moulded part for stiffness without making the main wall thicker. More
Right to repair
Rules and expectations that products can be repaired, for example through spare parts and repair information being available. More
Risk assessment
A documented review of the hazards a product could present and how they are controlled. More
RoHS
Rules restricting certain hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment. More
Root cause analysis
Working back from a reported symptom to the underlying reason it happened, so the fix removes the cause rather than the symptom. A common method is to keep asking why, sometimes called the five whys. More
Root sum of squares (RSS)
A statistical way of combining tolerances in a stack-up that assumes each varies randomly and independently. It predicts the range most assemblies will fall in, which is narrower than a worst-case analysis. More
Rotary encoder
A turning control that counts steps, often with detents you can feel, whereas a potentiometer gives a position. Its shaft needs a seal, and it should be held in the housing so side loads stay off the board. More
Route to market
How a product reaches customers: direct, marketplaces, retailers, distributors or business sales. More
Rules of origin
Rules that decide where goods come from for trade purposes, which can affect duty under trade agreements. More
Runner
The channel in a mould that carries molten plastic from the sprue to the gates. More
Runway
How long a business can operate before its cash runs out at the current burn rate. More

S

Safety factor
The allowable stress divided by the calculated stress: how much margin a part has. It is only as good as the allowable figure used; with the yield strength, a factor of 1 means the part is just starting to bend permanently. More
Sale or return
A trade arrangement where a retailer can return unsold stock to the supplier, which keeps the stock risk. More
Scrap rate
The share of parts or products rejected during manufacture. More
Screen printing
Printing that pushes ink through a fine mesh stencil onto a part, suited to flat or gently curved faces and larger areas of colour. More
Screw port
A channel built into an extruded profile, usually in the corners, that screws go straight into, so end caps can be fixed with no drilling or inserts. Agree its shape and size with the extruder and fastener supplier. More
Second moment of area
A property of a cross-section's shape and size, written I, that sets how well it resists bending; the material's elastic modulus sets the rest. For a flat bar it rises with the cube of the thickness, so doubling the thickness makes it eight times stiffer. More
Second source
An alternative supplier or interchangeable part that can replace a component if the first becomes unavailable, so one shortage does not stop production. More
Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme (SEIS)
A UK scheme offering tax reliefs to investors in very early-stage qualifying companies. More
Self-clinching fastener
A nut, stud or standoff pressed into a hole in sheet metal so that the metal flows into its grooves and locks it in place, giving a strong thread in sheet too thin to tap. More
Service loop
A little controlled slack left in a cable so a board can be lifted out and unplugged without straining it. It should be clipped so it cannot wander into a seam. More
Settlement discount
A small discount offered to a buyer for paying an invoice early. More
Sheet metal
Parts made by cutting, punching and bending flat metal sheet, such as enclosures and brackets. More
Shelf-ready packaging
Outer cases designed to open into a display tray, so staff can put the whole case on the shelf without unpacking each unit. More
Short shot
A moulding defect in which the plastic does not fill the tool, leaving the part incomplete, often at the end furthest from the gate or at thin features. More
Shrinkage
The reduction in size as plastic cools in the mould; tools are cut oversize to allow for it. More
Side action
A moving part of a mould tool that forms an undercut and slides out before the part is ejected. More
Silicone keypad
A moulded silicone rubber part carrying one or more buttons, with carbon pills underneath that press on contact pads on the circuit board. Clamped round its edge it is both button and seal, with a softer press than a tactile switch, but it needs its own mould. More
Silicone tooling
Soft moulds made from silicone around a master pattern, used for vacuum casting short runs. More
Sink mark
A shallow depression on a moulded surface caused by a thick section shrinking as it cools. More
SLA
Stereolithography: 3D printing that cures liquid resin with light, giving fine detail and smooth surfaces. More
SLS
Selective laser sintering: 3D printing that fuses nylon powder with a laser, producing strong functional parts. More
Snap fit
A moulded feature that clips parts together without separate fasteners. More
Sprue
The main channel through which plastic enters a mould from the machine nozzle. More
Staking
Fixing a part with a hole onto a plastic post by melting the tip of the post into a head, like a rivet, with a heated tool or ultrasonic vibration. Removing the part means cutting off the heads. More
Standard
A published document setting out agreed requirements or test methods, such as a British or European standard. More
Steel-safe
Describes a mould feature first cut so that any likely correction only means removing steel, which is much easier than adding it back. A hole might be cut slightly small so it can be opened up after measuring. More
STEP file
A neutral 3D CAD file format that most CAD systems can open, commonly used to share part models with suppliers. More
STL file
A common file format for 3D printing that describes a part's surface as a mesh of small triangles. It loses the exact geometry, so a STEP file is usually sent as well. More
Straight pull
A moulded part or feature that can come straight out of a two-part mould tool as it opens, with no side actions or lifters. Straight-pull parts make simpler, cheaper tools. More
Strain relief
A feature that takes the pull on a cable at the housing, so the force does not reach the connector or solder joints. More
Stress raiser (stress concentration)
A feature that concentrates stress, such as a sharp internal corner, the root of a boss or rib, a hole near an edge or a sudden change in wall thickness. Cracks tend to start there, so generous radii help. More
Supply agreement
A written agreement setting the rules for a whole relationship with a manufacturer: specification and quality, tooling, IP, pricing, orders, delivery, payment, changes, responsibility for faults and how the relationship ends. Each purchase order then only deals with quantities, dates and prices. More
Support structures
Temporary material printed under overhangs to hold them up while a part is 3D printed, then removed afterwards, usually leaving marks. Powder bed processes such as SLS and MJF do not need them. More
Surface finish
The texture and appearance of a part's surface, such as as-machined, polished, textured, bead-blasted or painted. Finer or specified finishes usually add cost. More
Surface preparation
Cleaning and treating a surface before bonding, painting or coating, for example by removing grease and mould release, abrading or priming, so the adhesive or coating holds. More

T

T1 samples
The first samples from a new mould sent to the customer to check against the drawing, before the tool is modified. Labels vary: many toolmakers call the very first shots T0, so agree what each label means. More
Tactile switch
A small switch on the circuit board that gives a crisp click after a short travel, usually pressed by a plastic button in the housing. It is made to be pressed straight down, so the button needs an end stop and the board needs support under it. More
Target cost
The most a product can cost to make and land while still meeting the business's margin at its price and route to market. More
Teardown
Taking apart a competitor's product to understand its parts, materials, manufacturing methods and likely cost. More
Technical file
The documentation that supports a product's conformity: design, risk assessment, standards and test reports. More
Temper
The condition an alloy is supplied in, such as soft, hard or fully heat-treated. The same alloy's strength differs considerably between tempers, so specify the temper as well as the alloy. More
Test fixture
A purpose-made rig that holds a part or circuit board in a repeatable position for testing or programming, often touching test points with spring-loaded pins. More
Test point
A pad or contact on a circuit board placed so that a fixture or probe can reach power, programming or key signals during testing and production. More
Thermal pad
A soft, heat-conducting pad that fills the gap between a warm component and a heatsink or case wall, giving the heat a path out. More
Thermal resistance
How strongly a heat path resists the flow of heat, in degrees Celsius per watt (°C/W): how many degrees warmer one end gets for each watt flowing through it. Resistances in a chain add, and the temperature rise is the power multiplied by the total. More
Thermoforming
Heating plastic sheet and forming it over a mould, used for trays, covers and large thin parts. More
Think-aloud
A usability testing method in which participants say what they are looking for, expecting and noticing as they work through tasks, so observers can see why they hesitate or make mistakes. More
Thread-forming screw
A screw designed to form its own thread in a plain hole in a plastic part, with no insert or tapping. The thread wears each time it is removed, so it suits joints opened occasionally. More
Title block
The box of information in the corner of an engineering drawing, giving details such as the part name and number, revision, material, scale and general tolerance. More
Tolerance
The permitted variation in a dimension. More
Tolerance band
The total range a dimension may vary within, from its lower limit to its upper limit. How the bands of two mating parts sit relative to each other decides the fit. More
Tolerance stack-up
The combined effect of tolerances on several parts on a fit or gap in an assembly.
Tool transfer
Moving a production tool, such as a mould, out of a supplier's factory to the owner or another supplier. Agreeing in advance how it can be collected, how much notice is needed and whether any costs apply, such as packing or outstanding invoices, keeps that route open. More
Tooling
The moulds, dies, jigs and fixtures needed to make parts in production. More
Tooling ownership
Who owns a production tool such as a mould, and so whether it can be moved to another supplier. It should be agreed in writing, with who pays for repairs and changes. More
Toolmaker
The company or specialist that designs and makes mould tools, and reviews part designs for mouldability before the tool is cut. More
TPE
Thermoplastic elastomer: a rubber-like plastic that can be moulded and over-moulded. More
Traceability
Being able to identify where and when a product or batch was made, and from which materials. More
Trade mark
A sign, such as a product or brand name or a logo, that tells customers whose product they are buying. It protects the brand, not the product's design or how it works; registration generally makes the rights clearer and easier to enforce. More
Trade mark attorney
A professional adviser who specialises in trade marks, including searches, applications and dealing with objections. More
Transit testing
Repeatable drop, vibration and compression tests that check packaging protects a product in shipping. More
Transition fit
A fit in which the hole and shaft tolerances overlap, so some pairs have a small gap and some a slight press. Used for accurate location where parts still need to come apart. More
Trim tool
A press tool that removes the runner, overflows and flash from die castings at volume. It is a separate cost from the die, so check it is in the quote. More

U

UK Trade Tariff
The official GOV.UK tool for finding commodity codes, duty rates and import rules. More
UKCA marking
The conformity marking used for many products placed on the market in Great Britain. More
UKNI marking
A marking used alongside CE marking for some products in Northern Ireland. More
Ultrasonic welding
Joining two plastic parts by pressing them together and vibrating them at high frequency until the interface melts and fuses. The joint is quick, strong and can seal, but cannot be undone. More
Undercut
A feature that stops a moulded part being pulled straight out of the tool. More
Unit cost
The cost to make one unit, including materials, process, labour, packaging and a share of tooling. More
Unit economics
The revenue, costs and profit attached to selling a single unit, used to show whether each sale makes money before overheads. More
Unit pack
The pack a shopper sees and buys, holding one product and carrying its own barcode. It is the smallest level of retail packaging. More
Usability testing
Watching representative users try to do realistic tasks with a prototype or product, without help, to find where they hesitate, make mistakes or work around the design. More
Use phase
The period when a product is in use, which dominates the environmental impact of many energy-using products. More

V

Vacuum casting
Casting polyurethane resin into silicone moulds under vacuum to make small batches of production-like parts. More
Validation
Checking with evidence that a product meets the needs of its users, or that an idea has real demand. More
Value engineering
A structured review of what each part, feature and process step does for the customer, looking for ways to deliver the same function at lower cost. More
Variable costs
Costs that rise with each unit made and sold, such as materials, assembly, packaging and fulfilment. More
Venture capital
Equity investment from funds in companies with potential for rapid, large-scale growth. More
Verification
Checking that a product meets its specified requirements, through tests, inspection and analysis. More
Voids
Internal air pockets in a moulded part, often caused by thick sections. More

W

Wall thickness
The thickness of a part's walls, which in moulding should be as even as possible. More
Warpage
Distortion of a moulded part as it cools unevenly. More
Warranty
A promise that a product, or a supplier's work, will meet an agreed standard for a stated time, with what happens if it does not, such as repair or replacement. A brand may give one to its customers, and a supplier may give one to the brand on the parts it makes. More
WEEE
Waste electrical and electronic equipment: the regulations that make producers responsible for electrical products at end of life. More
Weld line
A visible line where two flow fronts of plastic meet in a mould. More
Wholesale price
The price a retailer or distributor pays the brand for a product. More
Wiring harness
A made-up set of wires cut to length with connectors, terminals and any sleeving fitted, built by the supplier to a harness drawing. Each harness gets its own part number and revision on the bill of materials. More
Work instructions
Illustrated, step-by-step instructions for each assembly or test station, written so an operator new to the product can build it correctly at normal speed. More
Working capital
Cash tied up between paying for stock and being paid for it. More
Works-like prototype
A model that proves a product functions, without needing to look like the final design. More
Worst-case analysis
A tolerance stack-up method that adds tolerances directly, assuming every part is at its limit at once. It is safe but can demand very tight tolerances. More

Y

Yield
The share of parts or products that pass inspection first time. More

0–9

304 and 316 stainless steel
The two most common stainless steel grades. 304 is the general-purpose grade for kitchen equipment and indoor fittings; 316 adds molybdenum, which makes it much more resistant to pitting from chlorides such as salt water and sea air. More
3D printing
Building parts layer by layer from digital models, with no tooling; ideal for prototypes and complex low-volume parts. More
6063, 6060 and 6082 aluminium
Wrought 6000-series aluminium alloys used for extrusion. 6063 and 6060 extrude well and anodise to a good finish; 6082 is stronger but generally harder to extrude in thin, detailed sections, may not anodise to quite the same appearance, and is also common for machined parts. More
