How to use the knowledge base

The site has four kinds of page, and most questions are best answered by using two or three of them together.

  • Reference pages give a quick, citable answer: a definition in the glossary, a formula on the formulas page, or a side-by-side table such as the processes comparison.
  • Guides explain the trade-off behind a decision, with a worked example and the common mistakes. Start with the hub guide for the plan section you are in.
  • Tools run the numbers for your own product. The figures you enter stay in your browser, and you can collect them in the Product Plan Builder.
  • Checklists and readiness checks tell you whether you are ready for a big commitment, such as talking to a manufacturer or paying for tooling.

A good pattern is to read the quick answer, run the tool with your own figures, then read the guide to understand what the result means and what to check next. If you are not sure where you are, the idea to manufacture overview describes the seven stages, and the walkthrough follows one illustrative product through all of them.

How the content is written

Every guide and reference page is written or reviewed by Andrew Hunter, a product design engineer in Belfast, and carries a last-reviewed date. Rules of thumb are given as typical ranges with their assumptions stated, and worked examples are labelled as illustrative: they show the method, not a quote or a benchmark for your product. Where a topic depends on law, tax or regulation, the pages link to the official UK source and say so; they are general information, not legal, tax or certification advice. If you spot an error, get in touch and it will be corrected openly.

Practical rule: use the knowledge base to frame the decision and find the input that matters most, then test that input with a supplier quote, a prototype or a customer conversation.

FAQ

What is in the Orion knowledge base?

Reference pages (a glossary, an estimating formulas page, a manufacturing processes comparison and printable checklists), hub guides for the seven sections of a product plan, in-depth guides with worked examples, and free interactive tools that run the numbers for your own product.

Do I need to sign up to use the tools or guides?

No. Everything is free to read and use with no sign-up or email address. The figures you enter in the tools stay in your browser, as the privacy page explains.

Where should I start if I am new to hardware product development?

Read the idea to manufacture overview to see the seven stages, then follow the learning path for your role. The "start here by question" table on this page points to the right guide and tool for the most common early questions.

Are the figures in the guides real prices?

No. Worked examples use illustrative figures to show the method. Costs vary widely by product, volume, supplier and date, so use the tools with your own figures and confirm them with supplier quotes.

Can I cite or link to these pages?

Yes. The reference pages are written to be clear and citable. Please link to the page you used and treat it as general guidance to check against your own project data.
