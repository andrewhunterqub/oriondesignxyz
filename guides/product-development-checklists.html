Printable product development checklists

Six free, printable checklists for the main decision points in developing a physical product, each linked to the interactive tool or guide behind it. No sign-up needed.

Quick answer

Six printable checklists for the main decision points in developing a physical product, free to print and use, with no sign-up.

Use them before talking to a manufacturer, before committing to tooling, while preparing for certification, when reviewing moulded parts, when sending a quote request and before launch. Each links to an interactive version or a guide that explains the reasoning. To print, use your browser's print option; the checklists print with tick boxes.

Ready to talk to a manufacturer

Can a manufacturer quote from what you send, without guessing? Score yourself with Ready to Talk to a Manufacturer?

  • Do you have 3D CAD of the parts in a standard format such as STEP?
  • Do you have drawings showing materials, finishes and critical tolerances?
  • Is there a bill of materials listing every part and quantity?
  • Do you know realistic quantities for the first order and per year?
  • Do you have a target unit cost worked back from your price and route to market?
  • Has the design been tested in a prototype that works as intended?
  • Have you checked the design against the process's design-for-manufacture rules?
  • Do you know which regulations and markings apply to the product?
  • Is a confidentiality agreement ready to sign before you share files?
  • Have you decided what you will ask suppliers to quote, and on what delivery terms?

Ready to commit to tooling

Tooling locks in the design and the spending. Score yourself with Ready to Commit to Tooling?

  • Have production-intent parts passed testing against your requirements?
  • Is the design frozen, with changes now going through change control?
  • Have you done pre-compliance or formal compliance testing where required?
  • Has a moulder or toolmaker reviewed the parts for manufacturability?
  • Is there evidence of demand beyond the break-even volume for the tooling?
  • Have you compared bridge tooling or low-volume routes with production tooling?
  • Is the material and grade chosen and tested?
  • Are tool ownership, life, modifications and samples agreed in writing?
  • Can you fund the tooling and the first stock order together?
  • Is there time and budget for at least one round of tool modifications?

Certification readiness

Preparing for compliance testing and documentation (a self-check, not legal advice). Score yourself with the Certification Readiness Checklist

  • Have you listed the product regulations that apply (for example electrical safety, EMC, radio, toys)?
  • Have you identified the designated or harmonised standards you will test against?
  • Do you know whether you can self-assess or need a conformity assessment body?
  • Have you done pre-compliance testing on engineering prototypes?
  • Is formal testing planned with a laboratory, with time for a re-test?
  • Is there a documented risk assessment for the product?
  • Is the technical documentation being compiled as you go (drawings, test reports, standards list)?
  • Are labels, markings, addresses and English instructions designed?
  • Is there a way to keep production consistent with the tested design (change control, traceability)?
  • Have you covered producer responsibilities (waste electricals, batteries, packaging) where they apply?

Injection-moulded part review

Points to check on each moulded part before asking a toolmaker for a review. Explained in the injection moulding DFM checklist

  • Draft on every face that is parallel to the direction the mould opens
  • Walls as even in thickness as the design allows, with gradual transitions
  • Ribs thinner than the walls they support, to avoid sink marks
  • Bosses supported by ribs and cored out rather than solid
  • Internal and external corners radiused
  • Undercuts removed, or agreed with the toolmaker if side actions are needed
  • Gate, ejector pin and parting line positions acceptable on cosmetic faces
  • Critical dimensions and tolerances marked; general tolerances realistic for moulding
  • Material and grade chosen with shrinkage allowed for
  • Texture and colour specified, with draft suited to the texture

Quote request (RFQ) pack

What to send so suppliers can quote accurately and comparably. Build one with the RFQ Pack Builder

  • Covering note: what the product is and what you are asking for
  • 3D CAD in a standard format, such as STEP
  • 2D drawings with materials, finishes and critical tolerances
  • Bill of materials with quantities
  • Quantities: first order, annual volume and how they may change
  • Quality requirements: inspection, testing, cosmetic standard and samples
  • Packaging and labelling requirements
  • Compliance requirements the supplier must support
  • Delivery terms, location and target dates
  • What to quote: tooling, unit price at each quantity, lead times and payment terms
  • Confidentiality agreement and contact details

Launch readiness

Before the first units reach customers. Background in product liability basics

  • Price checked against every margin in the chain and VAT
  • Compliance confirmed, with technical documentation and markings complete
  • Instructions, warnings and labels final and checked by real users
  • Packaging tested for transit and sized for cartons and pallets
  • First-article inspection passed and production checks agreed
  • Batch or serial numbers in place for traceability
  • Product liability insurance in place
  • Returns, warranty and customer support process ready
  • Stock, fulfilment and delivery promises agreed
  • Listings, product pages and retailer information ready
  • Cash plan covers the period until sales income arrives
  • Packaging and producer responsibilities checked where they apply
Practical rule: a "partly" is a "no" until it is finished. The gaps you are unsure about are often the ones that cause delays.

FAQ

Are these checklists free to download?

Yes. They are free to use and print, with no sign-up or email required. Use your browser's print option to print them with tick boxes.

What is the difference between these and the readiness tools?

The printable lists are for meetings and paperwork. The interactive tools weight each question, give a readiness score and list the gaps in order of importance.

When should I use the tooling checklist?

Before paying for production tooling, once production-intent parts have been tested and you have evidence of demand beyond the tooling break-even volume.

Is the certification checklist legal advice?

No. It is a self-check to help you prepare. Confirm which regulations apply with official guidance and, where needed, a test house or notified body.
