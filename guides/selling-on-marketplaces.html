Selling on marketplaces: fees, fulfilment, listings and responsibilities

What to know before selling a physical product on an online marketplace: the full set of fees, whether to fulfil orders yourself, listings and reviews, keeping prices consistent across channels, and the responsibilities that stay with you.

Quick answer

Marketplaces give a new product instant reach, but fees, fulfilment, advertising and returns can take a large share of the price, and you stay responsible for the product.

Work out what you keep per sale after referral fees, fulfilment and storage, advertising and returns before listing. Choose between fulfilling orders yourself and using the marketplace's fulfilment service, prepare clear listings and compliance documents, and protect your pricing so it works across all your channels. Check each marketplace's current fees and rules, which change often.

Why sell on a marketplace

  • Reach: shoppers already searching for products like yours.
  • Trust: familiar checkout, delivery and returns.
  • Evidence: real sales and reviews early, which can help with retailers and investors.

The trade-offs are fees, less control over the customer relationship, price competition and dependence on one platform's rules.

What you will pay

Cost What to check
Referral or selling fees Usually a percentage of the sale price, varying by category; sometimes with a minimum per item
Account or subscription fees Monthly fees for professional seller accounts
Fulfilment and storage Per-unit fees if the marketplace stores and ships for you, plus storage charges that can rise for slow-moving stock
Advertising Sponsored listings are often needed to be seen, especially at launch
Returns Return shipping, damaged or unsellable returns, and refunds

Model these with the Target COGS by Route to Market tool, which includes a marketplace route.

Fulfilling yourself or using the marketplace

  • Self-fulfilment: more control and no storage fees, but you handle packing, delivery promises and returns.
  • Marketplace fulfilment: faster delivery options and less admin, but fees, preparation rules for inbound stock, and storage charges.

Packaging must survive parcel networks; see packaging design for shipping.

Practical rule: calculate what you keep per sale after every fee, advertising and returns, at a realistic price. If the marketplace route only works at full price with no advertising, it does not work.

Listings, reviews and pricing

  • Clear listings: accurate titles, real photographs, dimensions, materials, what is in the box and how to use it. If you also sell through shops, retail-ready packaging and barcodes covers barcodes and retail packs.
  • Honest reviews only: never buy or incentivise fake reviews; it is unlawful and breaches marketplace rules.
  • Consistent pricing: undercutting your retailers on a marketplace damages those relationships; see retail and distributor margins.

Your responsibilities

Selling through a marketplace does not move your legal responsibilities onto it. You still need a safe, compliant product with correct markings, labelling and documents, and marketplaces may ask for evidence such as test reports or declarations of conformity. Consumer rules on distance selling, cancellations and refunds apply; see online and distance selling for businesses and accepting returns and giving refunds. For product safety, see product certification basics and product liability basics.

Common mistakes

Pricing without all the fees. Fulfilment, storage, advertising and returns can turn a profitable price into a loss.
Sending too much stock. Slow-moving stock in marketplace warehouses can attract rising storage fees.
Relying on one platform. Account suspensions or rule changes can stop sales overnight.
Missing compliance documents. Listings can be removed if you cannot show the product meets the rules.

FAQ

Should I sell my product on a marketplace?

Marketplaces offer reach, trust and early sales evidence, but fees, fulfilment, advertising and returns can take a large share of the price. Calculate what you keep per sale before deciding, and avoid relying on one platform.

What fees do marketplaces charge?

Typically a referral or selling fee as a percentage of the price, account fees, optional fulfilment and storage fees, and advertising costs. Fees vary by marketplace and category and change often, so check current rates.

Should I use marketplace fulfilment?

It can give faster delivery and less admin, but adds per-unit and storage fees and inbound preparation rules. Self-fulfilment gives more control. Compare the full cost of both at your volumes.

Is the marketplace responsible for my product's safety?

Not instead of you. As the seller and usually the producer, you remain responsible for a safe, compliant product and correct information. Marketplaces may ask for evidence of compliance.

Do consumer return rules apply to marketplace sales?

Yes. Distance-selling rules, including cancellation rights and refunds, apply to online sales to consumers. Check current GOV.UK guidance on online selling and returns.
