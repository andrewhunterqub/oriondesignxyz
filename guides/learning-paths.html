Learning paths

By Andrew Hunter, Product Design Engineer · Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · For anyone who wants a guided route through the site rather than browsing

Learning paths: guided routes through product development for founders, engineers, spin-outs and students

Four guided routes through Orion Design's guides and free tools, each for a different reader. Follow one in order to build understanding step by step, and save tool results to your product plan as you go.

Quick answer

Pick the path that matches where you are, and work through it in order; each step builds on the last.

Each learning path is a short sequence of Orion Design guides and free tools, chosen for one kind of reader. Steps take from a few minutes to half an hour. Save tool results to your Product Plan Builder as you go, so the path leaves you with a plan as well as knowledge.

Choose a path

  • Engineers and designers
  • University spin-outs and researchers

First-time founders with a product idea

You have an idea for a physical product and want to know what it takes, what it costs and what to do first, without wasting money.

  1. Idea to manufacture: See the whole journey and where you are on it.
  2. Walkthrough: one product from idea to manufacture: Follow one illustrative product through every stage and see how the numbers connect.
  3. Opportunity and concept: Prove the problem before designing the product.
  4. How to validate a product idea: Gather evidence that people will pay.
  5. Product Purpose Framework (tool): Test whether the idea is clearly defined and worth making.
  6. How much it costs to develop a hardware product: Understand the full cost stack, not just design fees.
  7. Industrial design vs engineering design: Know which skills you need at each stage and how to brief them.
  8. Looks-like vs works-like prototypes: Know what each prototype must prove before you pay for it.
  9. Choosing a 3D printing process: Match each prototype round to the printing process that answers its question.
  10. Usability testing a prototype: Watch real people use the prototype before the design is fixed.
  11. Running a design review: Check the design at each gate and record what was decided.
  12. Timeline Planner (tool): Get a realistic first timeline to launch.
  13. Route to market and margins: Work back from the price to what the product can cost.
  14. Retail-ready packaging and barcodes: What retailers usually expect before the first order.
  15. Getting and comparing manufacturing quotes: Compare suppliers on landed cost and cash, not unit price alone.
  16. Supply agreements with a manufacturer: What to put in writing before tooling and the first order.
  17. Planning a pilot production run: Prove the process and packaging before the first full order.
  18. Funding and cash: Find out how much money you really need, and from where.
  19. Building a hardware financial model: Join cost, price, volume and cash into one model you can test.
  20. Product Plan Builder (tool): Collect your results into one plan you can share.

Engineers and designers

You can design the product; you want sharper decisions on process, cost, tolerances and manufacture, with numbers you can defend.

  1. Development and prototyping: Plan prototype rounds around the risks they retire.
  2. Planning electronics in a physical product: Design the board and enclosure together, with heat, EMC and test access in mind.
  3. Managing heat in a product enclosure: Estimate temperature rise and give the heat a path out.
  4. Enclosure Temperature Rise Estimator (tool): Check the hottest part against its target from power and the heat path.
  5. Choosing a 3D printing process: FDM, resin and powder processes compared for prototype rounds.
  6. Manufacturing and cost: Processes, cost drivers and tooling decisions in one place.
  7. Injection moulding explained: Tooling choices, lead times and what drives cost.
  8. Overmoulding and two-shot moulding: Soft grips, moulded-in seals and bumpers, and when they pay back.
  9. Choosing how to make an enclosure: Pick a route for the housing now, and plan how it changes as volume grows.
  10. Die casting explained: How metal parts are die cast, the design rules and when it pays back.
  11. Aluminium extrusions for product enclosures: Design an extruded profile, end caps and machining for a low-tooling enclosure.
  12. Injection moulding DFM checklist: The design rules that help prevent common moulding problems.
  13. From 3D-printed prototype to moulded part: What has to change before a printed design can be tooled.
  14. Surface finishes for plastic and metal parts: Choose and specify finishes, allowing for build-up and masking.
  15. DFM Checker (tool): Check walls, draft, ribs and bosses on your part.
  16. Tolerances and engineering drawings: Decide which dimensions need tight tolerances and what a quoting drawing must show.
  17. Bills of materials and revision control: Keep the BOM, CAD and drawings in step as the design changes.
  18. Joining methods: Screws, inserts, snap fits, welding and adhesives compared for cost, assembly and repair.
  19. Designing snap fits and living hinges: Check the strain in a snap hook and design hinges that last.
  20. Sealing and waterproofing an enclosure: Gaskets, O-rings, vents and IP ratings, designed in from the start.
  21. Bracket Deflection Calculator (tool): Estimate how far a bracket or beam bends and how stressed it is.
  22. Designing a product to survive drops and impacts: Design out the weak points, then drop-test every face, edge and corner.
  23. Designing buttons, switches and controls: Choose the control type, get the travel and feel right, and keep it sealed.
  24. Cables, wiring and connectors inside a product: Route, retain and connect internal wiring so it survives assembly, drops and service.
  25. Tolerance Stack-up Calculator (tool): Check critical fits worst case and statistically.
  26. Plastic Material Selector (tool): Shortlist materials against your requirements.
  27. Bill of Materials Cost Builder (tool): Roll up part costs into the unit cost.
  28. Getting and comparing manufacturing quotes: Normalise supplier quotes so the comparison is fair.
  29. Mould tool trials and sample approval: Read trial samples, agree tool changes and sign off a golden sample.
  30. Planning a pilot production run: What to measure, who attends and the go/no-go criteria.
  31. Assembly and work instructions: Write step-by-step instructions the production line can follow.
  32. Reducing the cost of an existing product: Value engineering from a cost breakdown, without breaking what customers value.
  33. After launch: returns, feedback and design changes: Find root causes and make controlled, re-tested changes.
  34. Engineering estimation formulas: The formulas behind the tools.

University spin-outs and researchers

You have technology that works in the lab and need to turn it into a product that customers, investors and regulators will accept.

  1. Opportunity and concept: Turn a technology into a product people need.
  2. Building a hardware business case: Show funders the product can pay its way.
  3. Building a hardware financial model: Turn the business case into monthly cash, break-even and a funding need.
  4. IP, partners and compliance: Protect the IP and understand the rules before disclosure.
  5. Trade marks and product names: Check and protect the name before it goes on packaging or into a tool.
  6. Product certification basics: Plan UKCA, CE and safety testing into development.
  7. Development and prototyping: From lab rig to production-intent design.
  8. Planning electronics in a physical product: Plan the move from lab hardware to a product board and enclosure.
  9. Funding routes in the UK and Northern Ireland: Grants, R&D relief, angels and spin-out routes.
  10. Investor Metrics and KPIs (tool): Prepare the numbers investors ask for.
  11. Ready to Talk to a Manufacturer? (self-check): Know when you are ready for suppliers.

Students and educators

You want a clear, structured picture of how physical products are developed and made, with worked examples to learn and teach from.

  1. Idea to manufacture: The stages of product development at a glance.
  2. Mapping the product design process: How design work is organised.
  3. How engineers choose between concepts: Structured concept selection with a Pugh matrix.
  4. Manufacturing and cost: Processes compared, with a worked cost example.
  5. Choosing plastic materials: Common polymers and their trade-offs.
  6. Choosing metals for a product: Aluminium, steel, stainless, brass and zinc compared.
  7. Sustainability and product life: Where a product's impact comes from.
  8. Route to market and margins: How sales channels shape the product.
  9. Glossary: Plain-English definitions of 160+ terms.

FAQ

Where should a first-time founder start?

With the founders' path: the Idea to manufacture overview, then the opportunity and validation guides, before spending on design. The path then covers cost, timeline, route to market and funding.

Are the learning paths free?

Yes. Every guide and tool on Orion Design is free to use, and tool results are saved only in your own browser unless you choose to share them.

How long does a learning path take?

Most steps take a few minutes to half an hour. A whole path can be read in an afternoon, but working through the tools with your own product's numbers is where the value is.

Can educators use these paths in teaching?

Yes. The students' path is designed as a structured introduction, and the guides include worked examples labelled as illustrative. Please link to the pages rather than copying them.
