Design registration vs patents: protecting how a product looks and works

An overview of the two main registered rights for physical products: what patents and registered designs each protect, how they compare, where unregistered rights fit, and why timing and disclosure matter. Take professional advice before relying on any of them.

Quick answer

A patent protects how something works; a registered design protects how it looks. Many products need to consider both, and timing matters for each.

Patents cover new, inventive technical features, such as a mechanism, process or function. Registered designs cover appearance: shape, contours, pattern, texture or ornamentation. Design registration is generally quicker and cheaper; patents are harder to get but can protect the underlying idea more broadly. In both cases, avoid public disclosure before filing, and take professional advice before relying on either. This guide is an overview, not legal advice.

Side by side

Patent Registered design
Protects Technical inventions: how a product works or is made Appearance: how a product or part looks
Main tests New, involves an inventive step, capable of industrial application New and has individual character
Process Detailed application, examination and usually several years to grant Simpler application, usually much quicker
Cost Higher, especially with attorney fees and overseas filings Usually lower
Maximum term in the UK Up to 20 years from filing, if renewal fees are paid Up to 25 years, renewed every 5 years
Good for A genuinely new mechanism, function or method A distinctive shape or look that copiers would imitate

Durations, fees and rules are set by each country and change over time; check the current details on GOV.UK for patents and registered designs.

Unregistered rights

Some protection for appearance and shape can arise automatically, without registration, through design right and, in more limited cases, copyright. Unregistered protection is generally narrower, shorter and harder to enforce, because you must prove copying and when the design was created. Keep dated records of design work either way.

Which do you need?

  • Does the product work in a new, non-obvious way? Discuss a patent with a patent attorney before showing it publicly.
  • Is the look what makes it sell? A registered design may be the most practical protection.
  • Both? Many products have a new feature and a distinctive look, and can be protected in both ways.
  • Neither? Speed to market, brand, trade marks, relationships with retailers and know-how may matter more than registration.
Practical rule: decide on protection before you publish, crowdfund, exhibit or pitch publicly. Disclosure before filing can destroy the novelty a patent needs, and grace periods for designs are limited and vary by country.

Timing and confidentiality

  • Use non-disclosure agreements when sharing details before filing; the NDA Generator creates a starting draft.
  • Plan filing dates around launches, trade shows and crowdfunding campaigns, using the Timeline Planner and Crowdfunding Planner.
  • Protection is territorial: decide which markets matter, including where products are made and sold.

Check you are free to sell

Owning a patent or design does not mean you are free to sell your product; someone else's rights may cover part of it. A freedom-to-operate review checks this. See how to protect product IP and the IP, partners and compliance hub.

Common mistakes

Showing it before filing. A public post, pitch or campaign can make a later patent impossible.
Patenting the look. Appearance is usually better protected by design registration.
Filing everywhere, or nowhere. Choose territories by where you will sell and where copies may be made.
Treating registration as a business plan. Protection only has value if you can afford to enforce it and the product sells.

FAQ

What is the difference between a patent and a registered design?

A patent protects a technical invention, meaning how something works or is made. A registered design protects appearance, such as shape, contours, pattern or texture. A product can have both.

Which is cheaper, a patent or a registered design?

Design registration is usually much cheaper and quicker. Patents involve a detailed application and examination, often with attorney fees, and cost more again when filed in several countries. Check current official fees.

Can I patent the shape of my product?

Shape and appearance are normally protected by design registration or unregistered design right (and, in limited cases, copyright) rather than patents. A shape may be part of a patent only where it gives a new technical effect.

Is my design protected without registering it?

Some protection can arise automatically through unregistered design right and copyright, but it is generally narrower, shorter and harder to enforce than a registered design. Rules differ between countries.

Should I file before launching or crowdfunding?

Yes, decide before any public disclosure. Disclosure before filing can prevent a patent, and grace periods for designs are limited. Take professional advice on timing.
