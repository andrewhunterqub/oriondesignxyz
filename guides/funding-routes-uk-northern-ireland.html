Funding routes for hardware start-ups in the UK and Northern Ireland

A practical guide to the main funding routes for hardware start-ups in the UK and Northern Ireland: what each needs, how long it takes, and how to combine them, with links to the official sources. It contains no invented figures; schemes and terms change, so always check the current details.

Quick answer

Match each funding route to the stage and the risk you are trying to retire, and start applications well before the money is needed.

Early technical work in the UK is commonly funded by founders, innovation grants and competitions, R&D tax relief and early angel investors (where SEIS and EIS reliefs can help). Launch is often funded by pre-orders or crowdfunding, start-up loans and angels; growth by equity rounds, stock finance and trading profits. In Northern Ireland, Invest NI is a central source of support, and university spin-outs have their own routes through technology transfer offices. Most routes take weeks to months to secure, so plan the timeline into the cash forecast. Schemes and terms change: check the official sources linked below. This is general information, not financial or tax advice.

Innovation grants and competitions

UK-wide, Innovate UK runs competitions for innovation funding; open competitions are listed on the Innovation Funding Service and on UKRI's funding finder. Applications are scored against published criteria, so read the scope and scoring guidance closely and answer every question directly.

  • Be specific about the innovation: what is technically uncertain, and why existing solutions do not solve it.
  • Show the market: who will buy, why, and the evidence you already have.
  • Plan and budget in detail: work packages, milestones, risks and a realistic budget.
  • Plan the cash: grants are commonly paid in arrears against claims, and many require a contribution from the company, so you still need working capital.

R&D tax relief

Companies spending on qualifying research and development can claim Corporation Tax relief, which can return part of that spending or provide a payable credit for some loss-making companies. Qualifying work must seek an advance in science or technology by resolving scientific or technological uncertainty; some hardware development meets that test and some does not. The rules have changed several times, so read HMRC's R&D relief guidance and take advice from an accountant who knows the current scheme. Keep records as you go: what was uncertain, what you tried, and what it cost.

Northern Ireland support

  • Invest NI's support for business covers innovation, research and development, skills and growth support for eligible businesses.
  • HBAN, the Halo Business Angel Network, connects early-stage companies with angel investors across the island of Ireland.
  • University spin-outs should talk to their institution's commercialisation or spin-out support; at Queen's University Belfast, QUBIS is the university's spin-out company.
  • The GOV.UK business finance and support finder lists grants, loans and support by location and sector.

Angel investment, SEIS and EIS

Angels invest their own money, often alongside others in a syndicate or network. Many UK angels look for companies that qualify for the Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme (SEIS) or the Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS), which give investors tax reliefs. Companies can apply to HMRC for advance assurance that they are likely to qualify, which many investors expect before committing. Eligibility conditions and limits apply and change, so check the current guidance.

For hardware, angels typically want to understand the unit economics by route to market, the cash low point, the tooling and certification plan, and what milestone their money buys. Hardware investor metrics that actually matter explains what to prepare.

Customer money: pre-orders and crowdfunding

Money from customers is often the cheapest funding for a first batch, and the strongest evidence of demand. It works best when you can show a convincing product, price it to cover the full landed cost, fees and shipping, and deliver on a date your manufacturing plan can actually meet. Delivery delays are not unusual in hardware, so build slack into the promised date and communicate early when things change.

Start-up loans and other debt

Start Up Loans are government-backed personal loans for starting a business, with mentoring support. Other options include bank lending, asset finance for equipment, and stock or invoice finance once the business is trading. Debt does not dilute ownership, but repayments start before many hardware products are profitable, so test them in the cash forecast.

Practical rule: start funding conversations at least two to three months before the cash forecast says you need the money, and longer for grants and equity. Late funding forces poor terms or cuts at the worst moment.

Combining routes

Many hardware businesses combine several sources. One possible pattern is founders' money and a grant for early technical work, R&D tax relief recovering part of that spend, pre-orders or an angel round for the first production batch, and stock finance or a larger equity round to grow. Check how sources interact: some grants restrict other public funding on the same costs, and investors will want to see the full funding plan.

Common mistakes

Applying for everything. Poorly targeted applications waste time; pick the routes that fit the stage and the project.
Forgetting match funding and arrears. A grant can still leave a cash gap until claims are paid.
No records for R&D relief. Reconstructing what was uncertain a year later is hard; keep notes as you work.
Raising equity too early. Before key risks are retired, investors price them in, which costs founders more of the company.
Relying on figures from old articles. Scheme limits and rates change; always check the current official guidance.

FAQ

What grants are available for hardware start-ups in the UK?

Innovation funding competitions run by Innovate UK are the main UK-wide source, listed on the Innovation Funding Service and UKRI's funding finder. Regional agencies such as Invest NI also offer innovation and growth support. Competitions open and close through the year, so check the official listings regularly.

Can a hardware start-up claim R&D tax relief?

It may, if it spends on work that seeks an advance in science or technology by resolving scientific or technological uncertainty. Some hardware development meets that test and some does not. The rules have changed several times, so read HMRC's guidance and use an accountant familiar with the current scheme. Keep records as you go.

What is SEIS advance assurance?

Confirmation from HMRC, requested by the company, that its planned share issue is likely to qualify for the Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme. Many angel investors expect it before investing because it gives them confidence of their tax relief.

How long does it take to raise money for a hardware product?

It varies by route. Start-up loans can take weeks; grants commonly take months from competition opening to funds and are paid in arrears; angel rounds often take several months from first meeting to money. Start well before the cash forecast says you need it.

Where can a Northern Ireland start-up get funding support?

Starting points include Invest NI, Innovate UK competitions, R&D tax relief, Start Up Loans, HBAN for angel investment across the island of Ireland, and, for university spin-outs, the institution's commercialisation or spin-out support, such as QUBIS at Queen's University Belfast.
