Right to repair and ecodesign rules: an overview for product designers

An overview of repair and ecodesign requirements for physical products: what they cover, how they differ between Great Britain, Northern Ireland and the EU, and the design decisions that prepare a product for them. Check official sources for your product group.

Quick answer

Repair and ecodesign rules are spreading from a few energy-using products to many more, so design products that can be repaired and documented, even where the law does not yet require it.

In Great Britain, ecodesign rules for some energy-related products already include requirements such as spare parts and repair information. In the EU, a broader ecodesign framework and a right-to-repair directive have been adopted, with detailed requirements set product by product over time. Northern Ireland follows some EU product rules. Requirements depend on the product category and market, so check the official sources. This guide is an overview, not legal advice.

What these rules cover

Type of requirement Examples
Energy and performance Minimum efficiency, standby power, product information
Repairability Spare parts available for a period, repair information for repairers, parts replaceable with common tools
Durability Minimum lifetimes or performance after use, for some categories
Information Labels, product information and, in the EU, a planned digital product passport for some products
Materials and recyclability Recycled content, substances of concern and design for recycling, where set for a product group

Great Britain

Ecodesign requirements in Great Britain apply to specific energy-related product groups, and for some of them include repair-related requirements such as spare parts availability and repair information for professional repairers. Check whether your product group is covered on GOV.UK: placing energy-related products on the UK market.

The EU and Northern Ireland

The EU has adopted the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, a framework that extends ecodesign beyond energy-related products, with requirements set for individual product groups over time, and the right-to-repair Directive (EU) 2024/1799, which strengthens repair obligations for certain products. Northern Ireland applies some EU product rules under the Windsor Framework, so products for NI or EU markets may face different requirements from those for Great Britain. Check which rules apply to your product and market, and take professional advice.

Practical rule: design as if repairability will be required. It is far cheaper to plan accessible fixings, spare parts and repair information at concept stage than to redesign later.

What designers should plan for

  1. Identify the parts most likely to fail and make them replaceable with common tools; see design for repair and disassembly.
  2. Keep a list of spare parts and decide how long you can supply them.
  3. Prepare repair information: exploded views, part numbers and procedures.
  4. Avoid software or design features that block genuine replacement parts without a safety reason.
  5. Record materials and substances so information requirements can be met.
  6. Track which product groups in your markets have, or are getting, requirements.

Common mistakes

Assuming it only affects big brands. Rules apply to products placed on the market, whoever makes them.
Treating GB, NI and EU as one market. Requirements and timings can differ.
Waiting for the law. Retrofitting repairability into a finished design is expensive.
Relying on summaries. Check the official text and current guidance for your product group.

FAQ

What are ecodesign rules?

Product requirements that set minimum environmental performance for specific product groups, such as energy efficiency, and for some products repairability, durability and information requirements.

Is there a right to repair in the UK?

In Great Britain, ecodesign rules for some energy-related products include repair-related requirements such as spare parts availability and repair information for professional repairers. Check GOV.UK guidance for your product group.

What is the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation?

Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, a framework that extends ecodesign beyond energy-related products. Detailed requirements, which can include durability, repairability and a digital product passport, are set for individual product groups over time.

Do EU repair rules apply in Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland applies some EU product rules under the Windsor Framework, so requirements can differ from Great Britain. Check current official guidance and take professional advice for your product.

How should designers prepare for repair rules?

Make likely-to-fail parts replaceable with common tools, plan spare parts and repair information, avoid blocking genuine parts without a safety reason, and record materials and substances.
