Quick answer

You are ready for certification when you know the rules and standards, have tested early, and are documenting as you go.

Readiness = Σ (importance × answer) ÷ Σ (importance × 2), where Yes = 2, Partly = 1, No = 0 Critical gap = a most-important question answered No

Knowing which regulations and standards apply carries the most weight, because everything else depends on them.

Method and assumptions

Each answer scores Yes 2, Partly 1, No 0, multiplied by the question's importance weight Readiness % = total score ÷ maximum possible score × 100 Gaps are ranked by weight × points missing

Assumptions

  • Importance weights reflect how much each step usually affects time and cost to compliance.
  • A critical gap is a top-weighted question answered No.

Limits

  • It is a self-check, not a compliance assessment or legal advice, and does not tell you which regulations apply.
  • Confirm requirements with official guidance and, where needed, a test house or notified body; see product certification basics.

How to read your result

  • About 80% or more with no critical gaps: ready to go ahead, with the remaining gaps closed in parallel.
  • About 55 to 80%, or one critical gap: possible, but with avoidable risk. Close the top gaps first if you can.
  • Below about 55%, or several critical gaps: too early. Work through the gaps in the order listed.

The weights and thresholds are Orion Design's rules of thumb for planning, not a formal standard. Each gap links to the guide or tool that helps close it. Save your result to your product plan and repeat the check as you progress.

FAQ

When should I start planning product certification?

At the requirements stage. The rules and standards can affect materials, enclosures, circuit design, labels and instructions, and pre-compliance testing on prototypes is far cheaper than redesign after a failed formal test.

Does this checklist tell me which regulations apply?

No. It checks whether you have worked that out and planned the steps. Product certification basics explains common UK regulations, and GOV.UK guidance covers each category.

What is a technical file?

The documentation that supports a product's conformity: a description, drawings and circuit diagrams, the risk assessment, the standards applied, test reports and the production controls that keep products consistent with what was tested.

Do I need a test laboratory?

For most electrical and radio products, formal testing is normally done at a laboratory, ideally one accredited for the relevant standards. Some products also need an independent conformity assessment body.

Is anything I answer stored?

No. The check runs in your browser and is only saved if you choose to save it to your product plan, which also stays in your browser.
