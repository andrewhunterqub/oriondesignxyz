Which signals matter most

Investors do not just look for revenue growth. They look for whether growth is buying time, strengthening margin, and creating a repeatable acquisition engine.

  • Runway shows how much time remains to prove the model.
  • CAC and payback show how efficiently demand becomes revenue.
  • Gross margin determines whether scale improves the business or just amplifies weakness.

Method and assumptions

Monthly revenue = units × average selling price Gross margin = (price − unit COGS) ÷ price Customers acquired = qualified leads × conversion rate CAC = marketing spend ÷ customers acquired CAC payback (orders) = CAC ÷ gross profit per unit Net burn = monthly burn − monthly gross profit Runway (months) = cash balance ÷ net burn

Assumptions

  • One unit per order and one average price and COGS.
  • Monthly burn is the total operating spend you enter; gross profit from sales offsets it.

Limits

  • It is a snapshot of one month, not a forecast; growth, seasonality and repeat purchases are not modelled.
  • Investors may define these metrics differently; state your definitions when you share them.

How to read your result

Look at the three signals together: gross margin, runway and how many orders it takes to recover the cost of acquiring a customer.

  • Healthy: gross margin is around 45% or more, runway is twelve months or longer, and acquisition cost is recovered within the first order. That combination suggests growth is buying time rather than burning it.
  • Borderline: margin sits somewhere in the 30% to 45% range, runway is between roughly six and twelve months, or it takes more than one order to recover acquisition cost. The business may work, but you need a clear plan for which number improves next and how.
  • Concerning: margin is below about 30%, runway is under six months, or acquisition cost is never recovered because gross profit per unit is too small. Prioritise cost reduction, pricing or a fundraise before spending more on growth.

Most hardware products are bought once, so recovering acquisition cost on the first order matters more than it does for subscription businesses. If you sell consumables or services alongside the device, note that separately, as this tool does not include repeat revenue. Save your result to your product plan to track how these metrics change as your costs and channels firm up.

FAQ

What gross margin do investors expect from a hardware product?

There is no fixed rule, but many hardware investors look for gross margins of roughly 40% or more at scale, particularly for direct sales; this tool treats about 45% or more as healthy. Lower margins can work with very high volumes or strong recurring revenue, but they leave less room for mistakes.

What is the difference between operating burn and net burn?

Operating burn is what the business spends each month on salaries, rent, development and marketing. Net burn subtracts the gross profit from sales, so it shows how quickly cash is actually falling. Runway in this tool is based on net burn.

How is CAC calculated here?

Customer acquisition cost is your monthly acquisition spend divided by the customers it produces, which the tool estimates from qualified leads multiplied by the conversion rate. If some sales come through retail without direct marketing spend, the figure will overstate or understate CAC, so treat it as a guide.

How often should I update these figures?

Monthly is a sensible rhythm once you are selling, and at least before every board meeting or investor conversation. The trend over several months usually tells a stronger story than any single snapshot.
