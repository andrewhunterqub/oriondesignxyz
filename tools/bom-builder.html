Quick answer

A bill of materials lists every part and quantity in a product; rolling up its costs gives the parts cost per unit.

Line cost = quantity per product × unit cost BOM cost = Σ line costs (grouped as material, processing, bought-in and packaging)

The BOM cost is not the full unit cost: assembly labour, scrap, freight and overhead come on top. The link to the Product Cost Estimator carries the four category totals across so you can add those.

Building a useful BOM

  • List every part, including fasteners, labels, adhesives, cables and packaging.
  • Use costs for your real order quantity. A quote for 100 parts and one for 10,000 can differ many times over.
  • Mark where each figure came from (quote, estimate or guess) in your own records, and firm up the biggest lines first.
  • Separate made and bought parts. Made parts have material and processing costs, and usually tooling; bought parts have a supplier price.

How to read your result

Look first at the largest lines: a few parts often make up most of the cost, so that is where design changes, alternative suppliers or volume pricing make the biggest difference. If bought-in components dominate, supplier choice and order quantity matter most; if made parts dominate, design for manufacture and the manufacturing route matter most (see the Manufacturing and cost hub and the DFM checker).

Then open the totals in the Product Cost Estimator to complete the unit cost, and add freight and duty with the Landed Cost Calculator if parts are imported. Save your BOM to your product plan.

FAQ

What is a bill of materials?

A structured list of every part, material and quantity needed to make one product. It is used for costing, purchasing, stock control and assembly, and it is one of the first documents a manufacturer will ask for.

Is the BOM cost the same as the product's unit cost?

No. The BOM cost covers parts and materials. The full unit cost adds assembly labour, scrap, testing, freight and duty, and a share of overhead and tooling. Use the Product Cost Estimator to add those.

What goes in the processing category?

The cost of turning raw material into a made part, such as moulding, machining, cutting, bending or finishing, when you are quoted for it separately from the material. If a supplier quotes a made part as one price, enter it as bought-in.

Why are only ten lines available?

The tool is for quick roll-ups. Group small items, such as all fasteners or all labels, into one line. For a full BOM with hundreds of lines, use a spreadsheet or product data system and bring the category totals here.

Are part names saved in the share link?

Yes, if you use the Copy link to this result button: the link carries any part names, categories, quantities and costs you have changed from the example, so anyone you send it to will see them. The address in your browser bar carries only the numbers and categories. Part names are also included in the copied summary and in the result you save to your product plan.
