Quick answer

Landed cost is the supplier price plus everything it costs to get the goods into your warehouse.

Customs value = goods + packaging + origin charges + freight + insurance Duty = customs value × duty rate Landed cost = customs value + duty + clearance + delivery + other (+ import VAT if not reclaimable)

Use the landed cost per unit, not the factory price, in margins, pricing and break-even. Import VAT is normally reclaimable for VAT-registered businesses, so it affects cash rather than cost.

How the calculation works

In the UK, duty is normally charged on the customs value, which is the price paid plus the cost of transport and insurance to the UK border. The calculator adds goods, packaging, origin charges, freight and insurance to get that value, applies your duty rate, then adds the costs after the border: customs clearance and port charges, delivery to your warehouse and anything else per shipment. Import VAT is calculated on the customs value plus duty. This is a simplified planning model: some costs after the border can also count towards the VAT value, and special procedures can change the figures, so confirm with a customs broker for real shipments.

How to read your result

  • Modest uplift (up to about 15 per cent): the import costs are a small part of the unit cost. Still use the landed figure in your margins.
  • Noticeable uplift (about 15 to 35 per cent): import costs matter. Check whether a different shipment size, delivery terms or freight mode would help, and confirm the duty rate.
  • Large uplift (above about 35 per cent): getting the goods here is a big part of the cost. Common causes are small shipments carrying fixed charges, air freight for heavy or bulky goods, or high duty. Compare with a nearer supplier on a landed basis.

These bands are a rule of thumb for interpreting the result, not industry benchmarks. FAQ

What should I enter if my supplier quotes a delivered price?

Enter the delivered price as the supplier price and put 0 for freight, insurance and any other costs the quote already covers. If the quote is DDP (delivered duty paid), set the duty rate and the import VAT rate to 0 as well, because under DDP the supplier normally pays the import duty and taxes.

Where do I find the duty rate for my product?

Find the commodity code for the product and look up its rate for the country of origin on the UK Trade Tariff on GOV.UK. Rates vary widely between codes, and a trade agreement can reduce the rate if the goods meet the rules of origin.

Why is import VAT shown separately?

VAT-registered businesses can normally reclaim import VAT, often by accounting for it on their VAT return, so it is not a cost to them, though it can affect cash. If you are not VAT-registered, choose that option and the VAT is included in the landed cost.

How can I reduce landed cost?

Larger or less frequent shipments spread fixed charges, sea freight is usually cheaper than air for non-urgent goods, smaller and lighter packaging reduces freight, and checking for trade-agreement preferences can reduce duty. Each option has trade-offs in cash tied up and lead time.

Is this calculator suitable for customs declarations?

No. It is a planning tool for estimating unit cost. Customs declarations must follow HMRC rules on classification and valuation; use a customs broker or the official guidance for real shipments.
