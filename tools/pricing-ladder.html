Quick answer

Each seller in the chain adds its margin on top of the price it paid, so the shelf price is often several times the landed cost.

Price needed for a margin m = cost ÷ (1 − m) Your price = landed cost ÷ (1 − your margin) Distributor's price = your price ÷ (1 − distributor margin) Retail price before VAT = previous price ÷ (1 − retailer margin) Shelf price = retail price before VAT × (1 + VAT rate)

Margins here are shares of each seller's selling price, the way retailers and distributors usually quote them. A 50% margin doubles the price; a 45% margin multiplies it by about 1.8.

How to read your result

  • Shelf price at or below target: the chain works at that price. Check it still covers promotions, returns and marketing.
  • Close to the target: little room for discounts or cost increases; test a pessimistic landed cost.
  • Above the target: reduce landed cost, shorten the chain (for example sell to retailers directly), accept a lower margin, or reposition the product at a higher price.

The multiple of landed cost is a quick sense check when comparing channels. For the reverse calculation and selling costs such as fulfilment and returns, use Target COGS by Route to Market; for the full picture, read Route to market and margins. Save your ladder to your product plan.

FAQ

What is a pricing ladder?

The sequence of prices a product passes through on its way to the customer: your selling price, any distributor's price, the retailer's price and the shelf price with VAT. Each step adds that seller's margin.

Why is the shelf price so many times the cost?

Because margins compound. If you, a distributor and a retailer each need a healthy margin on their own selling price, and VAT is added at the end, the shelf price is commonly several times the landed cost.

Should I use margin or markup?

This tool uses margin, as a share of the selling price, because that is how retailers and distributors usually state their terms. A 50% margin is the same as a 100% markup. Always confirm which one a partner means.

Does the ladder include selling costs such as fulfilment and returns?

No; it shows the price chain only. Your margin needs to cover your selling costs and overheads. Target COGS by Route to Market includes those selling costs explicitly.

What VAT rate should I use?

The UK standard rate is 20%, which applies to most products; some goods have reduced or zero rates. Check GOV.UK for your product, and use 0 if you are pricing for business customers who see prices without VAT.
