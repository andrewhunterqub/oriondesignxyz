When to use this

Use the NDA generator to prepare early product and supplier conversations.

The draft is intended to help structure confidentiality before sharing product designs, CAD files, drawings, prototypes, supplier quotes, commercial plans, or manufacturing assumptions. It is a starting point, not legal advice.

What the draft does

The generated text sets out the agreement type, parties, purpose of disclosure, confidential information, core obligations, exclusions, term, return or destruction language, and governing law.

For a supplier discussion, keep the purpose narrow enough to match the real conversation: evaluating manufacturing feasibility, cost, quality, and production route for a defined product. Avoid using an NDA as a substitute for careful staged disclosure.

How to read your result

Important: the generated text is a starting point for discussion, not legal advice and not a finished agreement. It has not been reviewed for your circumstances, and a qualified solicitor should check it before anyone signs.

The summary cards confirm the three choices that shape the agreement most: whether it is one-way or mutual, how long the confidentiality obligations last, and which country's law applies. The draft itself sets out who is sharing with whom, why, what is covered, what is excluded, and what happens to the information afterwards.

  • Looks right: the parties are correctly named legal entities, the purpose describes the actual conversation, and the information listed matches what you plan to share. A term of two to five years is common for early product discussions.
  • Worth checking: a one-way NDA when the other party will also share drawings or pricing usually should be mutual. A very broad purpose or a vague list of information can make the agreement harder to rely on.
  • Get advice first: if you are sharing something that would be hard to protect any other way, such as a process or formulation you plan to keep secret, if the other party is overseas, or if they send back their own terms, have a solicitor review before disclosing anything.

A solicitor may also add clauses this draft leaves out, such as remedies for breach, confirmation that no licence or ownership is transferred, and how the agreement can be ended. Keep a record in your product plan of who has signed what and when, so disclosure stays staged and traceable. If you are unsure what to share with a manufacturer at each stage, Andrew can talk you through it from an engineering point of view.

What to do next

  • Check that the parties are named correctly before sending anything.
  • Keep the purpose specific to the conversation you are actually having.
  • Share only the information required for the next decision.
  • Have the agreement reviewed for your jurisdiction, project risk, and commercial context.

FAQ

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a drafting aid for early product and manufacturing conversations. Have the final agreement reviewed by a qualified legal professional.

Does an NDA mean it is safe to share everything?

No. Use staged disclosure. Share only what the other party needs to answer the next useful question.

When should I ask someone to sign an NDA?

Before you share confidential information that is not already public, such as unfiled designs, technical know-how, test results or detailed business plans. A first conversation can often stay at a high level without one, which lets you check whether the relationship is worth pursuing before sharing the detail.

Does an NDA protect my idea in the same way as a patent?

No. An NDA is a contract with the people who sign it. It does not stop someone else from developing a similar product independently, and it does not give you rights against third parties. Registered rights such as patents and registered designs work differently; the IP protection guide explains the options.

What if a manufacturer asks me to sign their NDA instead?

That is common, particularly with larger suppliers. Read it carefully: check that it is mutual if you are both sharing information, that the definition of confidential information covers what you will share, and that the duration and permitted uses are reasonable. If the terms matter to your business, ask a solicitor to review them.
