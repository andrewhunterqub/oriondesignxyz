Quick answer

Most hardware products take many months from idea to launch; the variables are prototype rounds, certification and tooling.

Stage weeks = typical range, positioned by complexity (or your own figure) Prototype weeks = weeks per round × rounds Launch preparation overlaps the second half of industrialisation Planned duration = end of last stage × (1 + contingency)

The typical ranges match the stage durations on the Idea to manufacture page. They are planning ranges, not quotes, and your own estimates should replace them as soon as you have them.

A simple product is placed near the bottom of each range, a regulated one at the top. The "quick to cautious" range shows the whole timeline at both ends, with contingency on the cautious end.

How to read your result

  • Use the planned duration for commitments such as launch dates and funding, not the quick end of the range.
  • Look at the longest stage: that is usually where better planning, fewer rounds or earlier decisions save the most time.
  • Revisit the plan at each stage gate and replace typical ranges with your own estimates and supplier lead times.

FAQ

How long does it take to develop a hardware product?

It varies widely. A simple mechanical product can go from idea to launch in well under a year, while connected or regulated products commonly take considerably longer. Prototype rounds, certification and tooling are the biggest variables, so plan your own stages rather than relying on an average.

Where do the stage durations come from?

They are typical planning ranges, consistent with the stage durations on the Idea to manufacture page. They are not quotes. Replace them with your own estimates and supplier lead times as soon as you have them.

How many prototype rounds should I plan?

Many products need two or three rounds before production-intent parts; complex or regulated products often need more. Plan rounds around the questions that must be answered and allow for at least one you cannot yet name.

How much contingency should a hardware timeline have?

Hardware timelines often slip because of failed tests, tool modifications and supplier delays. A contingency of around 15 to 25 per cent is a common starting point, with more for novel technology or a first product.

Can stages overlap?

Yes, and they usually do. The planner overlaps launch preparation with industrialisation. You can shorten other stages by overlapping work, but overlapping tooling with unfinished testing increases the risk of costly changes.
