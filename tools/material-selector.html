Quick answer

Shortlist from requirements, then confirm with real grades and real tests.

Match % = Σ (importance × rating) ÷ Σ (importance × 5) Essential = rating of 4 or 5 required, otherwise the material is ruled out

The ratings compare typical unfilled general-purpose grades with each other. They are Orion Design's judgement for shortlisting, not values from a standard or datasheet.

The ratings used

1 is poor and 5 is excellent; for cost, 5 means cheapest. Fillers, additives and specific grades can move any of these considerably.

How to read your result

  • Several good matches: ask suppliers for datasheets of specific grades of the top two or three, and compare price and availability.
  • Only one or two: your essentials are demanding. Check each is truly essential, and look at filled or modified grades.
  • None: relax an essential, consider a specialist grade, or reconsider the design, for example by protecting a part from UV or heat rather than asking the material to cope.

Regulatory needs, such as flame ratings for electrical enclosures or food-contact suitability, depend on specific grades and must be checked separately. Choosing plastic materials explains the trade-offs in more depth, and the Injection Moulding DFM Checker uses typical wall thicknesses for each material. Save your shortlist to your product plan.

FAQ

Where do the material ratings come from?

They are Orion Design's relative ratings for typical unfilled general-purpose grades, based on widely known properties of each plastic family. They are a shortlisting aid, not datasheet values, so confirm with the data for the specific grade you plan to use.

Why is my preferred material ruled out?

A criterion marked Essential requires a rating of 4 or 5. If a material is rated 3 or lower on that criterion, or does not meet the transparency or feel filter, it is ruled out. Change Essential to Important to keep it in the ranking.

Does the selector include glass-filled grades?

No. Fillers such as glass fibre raise stiffness and heat resistance considerably, especially for nylon, but reduce toughness and increase tool wear. If a filled grade could be suitable, discuss it with your material supplier.

Can it tell me if a plastic is food-safe or flame-rated?

No. Food-contact suitability and flammability ratings belong to specific grades, not plastic families. Ask suppliers for the relevant declarations or test data for the grade you choose.

Should I choose the cheapest suitable material?

Not automatically. Material is only part of part cost: a material that moulds faster, needs no painting or avoids failures in use can be cheaper overall. Compare the whole cost, not just price per kilogram.
