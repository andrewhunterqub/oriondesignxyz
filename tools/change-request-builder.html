Quick answer

An engineering change request records what should change, why, which parts it affects, what it touches, who approved it and from when it applies.

Request → impact check → approval → release → effectivity

A small team needs the same steps as a large company, not the overhead: a numbered request like this one, a change log and a folder per change is enough. The form, fit and function answer decides whether the changed part gets a new revision or a new part number.

How to use the change request

  1. Raise the request as soon as a change is proposed. Give it a number and list every affected part number with its current revision.
  2. Answer the form, fit and function question, then work through the impact check. Use your indented BOM to find every assembly that contains the part.
  3. Ask the named approver to decide, and record the decision and the date.
  4. Once approved, update the CAD, drawing and BOM together, release the new revision or new part number, and mark the old revision obsolete or "use up existing stock".
  5. Set the effectivity, tell suppliers in writing and ask them to confirm. Print the request or save it to your product plan and keep it with the change log.

How to read your result

  • Change type follows your form, fit and function answer: interchangeable parts get a new revision, parts that are not interchangeable get a new part number.
  • Impact areas checked counts stock, tooling, certification and testing, cost, suppliers and documents. "Not checked yet" is honest; it just means the request is not ready for a decision.
  • Still to complete lists gaps and contradictions, such as an interchangeable change with a new part number, or an approval with no effectivity.

Assumptions and notes

  • The steps and terms follow the bills of materials and revision control guide: revisions as letters (A, B, C), three release states (draft, released, obsolete) and the form, fit and function test.
  • The tool does not decide whether a change is interchangeable, what it will cost or whether test results still hold. It records your answers and points out gaps; the judgement stays with your team.
  • If a customer, certification body or contract asks for its own change notification, follow that as well. This tool does not check those requirements.
  • The worked examples use the illustrative bike light from the guide. The part numbers, revisions and serial numbers are invented to show the layout.
  • Your inputs are kept in your own browser so they are still there when you come back; nothing is sent to a server.

Faults found after launch follow the same steps, with root cause and re-testing added; after launch: returns, feedback and design changes covers that side.

FAQ

What is an engineering change request?

A short, numbered record of a proposed change to a released part: what should change, why, and which part numbers are affected. It then carries the impact check, the approval decision and the point from which the change applies, so the whole history of the change is in one place.

When does a change need a new part number instead of a new revision?

When the old and new parts are no longer interchangeable in form, fit or function. If the new part can replace the old one everywhere without any other change, raise the revision; if mixing them could cause a problem, give the new part its own number, and raise the revision of the parent BOM too.

What does effectivity mean?

It is the point from which a change applies, given as a date, a production batch or a serial number. Recording it means you can always tell which units contain the old part and which contain the new one.

Is what I type stored anywhere?

Not on a server. The change request is generated in your browser, and your inputs are remembered in your own browser's storage so they are still there when you come back; clearing this site's data removes them. The request is added to your product plan only if you choose to save it there, and the plan also stays in your browser.
