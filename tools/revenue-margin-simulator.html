Quick answer

Launch margin is what remains after route deductions, COGS, hidden selling costs, returns, and overhead are counted.

Operating profit = annual contribution after variable costs - annual overhead

If a launch only works before marketplace fees, retail terms, packaging, fulfilment, and overhead are included, it is not commercially ready.

What the planner helps you see

Launch economics often look fine until you add the cost stack that sits between the customer and the factory. This planner shows how route mix, hidden selling costs, and annual overhead alter the real margin picture.

A product can look profitable at list price and still disappoint once marketplace fees, retailer terms, secondary packaging, warehousing, and fulfilment are layered in. That is why route choice and cost structure need to be reviewed together.

Questions to ask after the result

  • Is the route mix giving away too much of the retail price?
  • Are we undercounting fulfilment, packaging, or account-management costs?
  • Does the product need a lower COGS or a different launch route to become resilient?

Method and assumptions

Price after VAT = RRP ÷ (1 + VAT rate) Kept per route = price after VAT × (1 − route fee or retailer margin) Contribution per unit = kept − COGS − route costs − returns reserve Annual contribution = Σ (annual units × route share × contribution per unit) Operating profit = annual contribution − annual fixed overhead

Assumptions

  • Channel shares are normalised so they add up to 100%.
  • Route costs per unit are the figures you enter; the returns reserve is a percentage of the amount kept.
  • One average price and COGS apply across all routes.

Limits

  • It does not model timing, payment terms, promotions, price changes or volume discounts.
  • Marketplace and retailer terms vary; replace the defaults with quoted figures.

How to read your result

Start with operating profit and the break-even volume, then look at contribution per route to see which part of the mix is helping or hurting.

  • Comfortable: operating profit is positive at the volume you can realistically sell, break-even sits comfortably below that volume, and every route makes a positive contribution per unit.
  • Borderline: the plan is close to break-even, or one route has a very thin or negative contribution that the others are carrying. Consider whether that route is worth its cost at launch or better added later.
  • Worrying: operating profit is negative and break-even is well above your forecast volume. Look first at unit COGS and the most expensive route, rather than assuming higher volume will close the gap.

It is normal for retail to carry the lowest contribution per unit, as it trades margin for reach. The question is whether the volume it brings is enough to justify it. Save your result to your product plan so the launch margin sits alongside your cost, route and cashflow assumptions.

If the plan only works with a COGS figure that has not yet been confirmed by a manufacturer, a second opinion is worthwhile. You can get in touch with Andrew to talk it through.

FAQ

Why plan launch margin by route to market?

Because DTC, marketplace, and retailer routes keep very different amounts of the retail price once fees, logistics, and selling costs are counted.

What hidden costs damage launch margin most often?

Warehousing, packaging, fulfilment, marketplace commissions, retailer margin, site or payment fees, and undercounted operating overhead are the usual leaks.

What gross margin should a hardware product aim for?

It varies by category and channel, but many hardware businesses aim for a gross margin of roughly 40–60% on the price they actually receive, so there is room for marketing, overheads and profit. Products sold mainly through retail often see lower margin per unit because the retailer's share comes off the top.

How can I reduce the impact of channel fees on margin?

Price for each channel rather than using one price everywhere, reduce packaging and fulfilment cost per unit, sell bundles or higher-value variants through high-fee channels, and design for a lower COGS from the start. The Target COGS tool shows the cost ceiling each route allows.

Should VAT be included in the retail price I enter?

Yes. Enter the shelf price including VAT, as UK customers see it, and set the VAT rate. The planner removes VAT before working out margin, because VAT is collected for HMRC rather than earned by the business. Forgetting to remove it is one of the most common reasons launch margins look better on paper than in practice.
