Quick answer

Most moulding problems come from a few features: uneven walls, missing draft, thick ribs and bosses, sharp corners and undercuts.

This checker compares your values with common rules of thumb and explains each result. It is a first pass, not a substitute for the moulder's DFM review or a mould-flow analysis.

The rules it uses

  • Wall thickness: compared with an indicative range for the chosen material, for unfilled grades.
  • Uniformity: thickest section up to about 1.25 times the nominal wall is treated as uniform; beyond about 1.75 times, sink and voids become likely.
  • Draft: at least about 1 degree for smooth faces and about 3 degrees for textured faces as a starting point; 0.5 to 1 degree on a smooth face is flagged to check, as it can suit shallow features.
  • Ribs: about 40 to 60 per cent of the wall thick, and up to about three times the wall tall.
  • Bosses: outside diameter around twice the hole, with a boss wall no more than about 60 per cent of the main wall.
  • Inside corners: radius at least about half the wall.
  • Undercuts: each one usually needs a moving part in the tool.

How to read your result

  • OK: the feature follows common guidance. It may still need adjusting for your material grade, flow length or cosmetic requirements.
  • Check: not necessarily wrong, but worth discussing with the moulder before the tool is designed.
  • Fix: likely to cause sink marks, warping, sticking, cracking or extra tool cost. Change it on the CAD model now, while it is cheap.

The thresholds are rules of thumb collected from common practice, not limits from a standard. Different materials, grades and part sizes justify different values, so always confirm with the material datasheet and your moulder. FAQ

Can this checker replace a moulder's DFM review?

No. It catches common problems early, but a moulder's review considers the specific tool, gate positions, material grade, flow length and cosmetic requirements, and may include a mould-flow simulation. Use the checker to arrive with a better design.

Where do the material wall thickness ranges come from?

They are indicative ranges of the kind commonly quoted for unfilled grades. The right wall for your part depends on its size, flow length and grade, so check the material supplier's datasheet and ask your moulder.

What counts as an undercut?

Any feature that stops the part being pulled straight out of the tool in the opening direction: side holes, clips and hooks facing sideways, internal grooves and threads, for example. Each usually needs a side action, lifter or other moving part.

Why does the checker treat textured surfaces differently?

Texture creates tiny undercuts in the surface, so textured faces need more draft to release without scuffing. Texture suppliers usually specify a minimum draft for each texture depth.

What should I measure as the thickest section?

The thickest solid area of plastic, such as the base of a solid boss, a junction where several walls meet, or a thickened edge. Measuring the diameter of the largest circle that fits inside the section is a practical way to find it.
