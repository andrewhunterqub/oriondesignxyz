Belfast & Northern Ireland

Product design and prototyping in Belfast, Northern Ireland.

Orion Design is a product design engineering consultancy based in Belfast, run by product design engineer Andrew Hunter. I help Northern Ireland start-ups, manufacturers, university spin-outs and established engineering businesses take physical products from idea to manufacture: concept development, prototyping, design for manufacture and production strategy.

Local, when it matters

Meet in person for the parts that need hands and eyes.

Some decisions are much easier in the same room: handling an early prototype, walking a production line, reviewing a part with a supplier, or watching someone use the product. Being based in Belfast makes that straightforward for teams anywhere in Northern Ireland, from Belfast and Lisburn to Derry~Londonderry, Newry and Enniskillen.

Remote, when it does not

CAD reviews, calls and shared prototypes work anywhere.

Much of product design engineering happens through shared CAD, drawings, test results and supplier quotes. I have worked with clients and manufacturing partners in Northern Ireland, Great Britain, the Republic of Ireland, France, Germany, Sweden, Poland, the United States, China, Taiwan, India, Australia and New Zealand, as well as companies with a global presence. The MLT Industries case study follows a product from concept into manufacture with the client in Australia.

Prototyping in Northern Ireland

Prototypes that answer the riskiest questions first.

Prototyping is where many projects start. The useful question is not “can we make one?” but “what does this prototype need to prove?”: that the product looks and feels right, that the mechanism works, that it survives testing, or that it can be made at volume. Planning each round around one question keeps the number of rounds, and the cost, down. The guide to how many prototypes you need sets out the usual sequence.

Choosing the route

The right process for each round.

For each round I prepare the CAD and match the process to the question: 3D printing for quick form and fit checks, CNC machining for functional parts in real materials, and vacuum casting for small batches of production-like parts. Being based in Belfast means early prototypes can be handled and reviewed together in person; later rounds and supplier samples can be reviewed the same way or remotely. The Prototype Strategy Tool compares the routes for your own part, and prototyping vs production explains when to move on to production processes.

Questions about working with a Belfast product designer

Where is Orion Design based?

Orion Design is based in Belfast, Northern Ireland. In-person meetings, prototype reviews and supplier visits are easy to arrange across Northern Ireland, and most work with clients elsewhere happens remotely.

Can you help with prototyping in Northern Ireland?

Yes. Prototyping is part of concept and development work: planning which prototypes answer which questions, preparing CAD for 3D printing, CNC machining or vacuum casting, and reviewing prototypes in person in Belfast or remotely. The Prototype Strategy Tool and the guide to how many prototypes you need are good places to start.

Do you work with clients outside Northern Ireland?

Yes. Clients and manufacturing partners have included businesses in Great Britain, the Republic of Ireland, France, Germany, Sweden, Poland, the United States, China, Taiwan, India, Australia and New Zealand, as well as companies with a global presence. Shared CAD, video calls and posted prototypes cover most of the work, with travel arranged when a visit genuinely helps.

Is Orion Design a product design agency?

It is a focused product design engineering consultancy rather than a large agency. You work directly with Andrew Hunter, who has worked in product design since 2010 and holds an MEng in Product Design and Development from Queen's University Belfast.

What kinds of products do you design?

Physical products: consumer products and accessories, scientific and medical devices, industrial equipment, enclosures, plastic parts, sheet-metal and soft-goods products. The emphasis is on making them work, and making them manufacturable at the right cost.

How do I get started?

Send a short description of the product, where it is now and what you need to decide next. You do not need drawings or a finished specification; shaping the brief is part of the work.

Developing a product in Northern Ireland?

Tell me where the product is and what is holding it back. I will reply with a suggested next step, whether that is a quick review, a prototype plan or a fuller project.
